What is the Difference Between Mean, Median, and Mode?

In statistics, these three terms are used to describe the "center" of a dataset. They are known as Measures of Central Tendency.

1. Mean (The Average)

The mean is the most common type of average. To find the mean, you add all the numbers together and divide by the total count of numbers. For example, the mean of 10, 20, and 30 is (10+20+30)/3 = 20.

2. Median (The Middle Value)

The median is the middle number when the data is arranged in order from smallest to largest. If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle numbers. The median is very useful because it is not affected by extreme "outlier" numbers (like a billionaire in a list of normal salaries).

3. Mode (The Most Frequent)

The mode is simply the number that appears most often in your list. A dataset can have one mode, multiple modes (if several numbers tie for the highest frequency), or no mode at all (if all numbers appear exactly once).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between mean, median, and mode?

Mean is the mathematical average. Median is the middle number in a sorted list. Mode is the number that appears most frequently.

Can I use negative numbers?

Yes. You can enter negative numbers like -10, -20, etc. The calculator will handle them correctly.

What happens if all numbers are the same?

If all numbers are the same (e.g., 5, 5, 5), then the mean, median, and mode will all be exactly that number (5).

How do I clear the calculator?

Just click the "Clear" button to remove all numbers and start a new calculation.
