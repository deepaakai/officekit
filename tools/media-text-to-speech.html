Text to Speech — Frequently Asked Questions

Everything about converting text into speech — voices, languages, quality, and privacy.

How do I use this Text to Speech tool?

Using the tool takes just four simple steps. First, type or paste your text into the large box at the top — up to 5,000 characters per session. Second, choose a voice from the "Voice" dropdown — the list shows all voices installed on your device, including different languages and accents. Third, optionally adjust the speed slider (0.5× to 2×) and pitch slider (0 to 2). Fourth, click the "Speak" button. The tool begins reading your text aloud immediately. You can pause anytime, resume, or stop completely. When done, click "Clear" to empty the box and start fresh. Everything happens instantly with no waiting, no signup, and no cost.

Is this Text to Speech tool free?

Yes — 100% free with no hidden costs. There's no signup, no email, no payment, no character limit (within the 5,000-character session cap), and no "pro" version. You can use it as many times as you want, for personal, educational, or commercial purposes. The tool is supported by lightweight ads elsewhere on the site, which is how we keep it free while protecting your privacy. We don't sell your text, we don't log what you type, and we don't ask for your email to unlock features. Every voice available on your device is available here at no charge.

Is my text sent to any server?

No — your text never leaves your device. The Text to Speech tool uses the browser's built-in SpeechSynthesis API, which relies on voices installed locally on your operating system. When you click "Speak," your browser processes the text entirely offline and generates the audio through your device's speakers or headphones. No text, no voice data, and no metadata is transmitted over the internet. You can prove this yourself: type your text, disconnect Wi-Fi, and click "Speak" — it will still work perfectly. This makes DocEasy the safest choice for reading sensitive documents, confidential notes, or private messages aloud.

How many voices are available?

The number of voices depends entirely on your device and operating system. Windows 10/11 typically offers 5–15 voices, including Microsoft David, Zira, Mark, and various language packs. macOS and iOS usually offer 20–50 voices, with high-quality voices like Samantha, Alex, Daniel, and multilingual options. Android offers fewer built-in voices but more can be installed through Google's TTS engine. Chrome OS offers 3–10 voices. The tool automatically lists every voice your device supports — just open the dropdown and pick your favorite. If you need more voices, install additional language packs from your OS settings, then refresh the page.

Why can't I hear anything when I click Speak?

If the Speak button seems to do nothing, check these common causes. First, make sure your device volume isn't muted — check the system volume slider. Second, check the browser tab isn't muted — right-click the tab and look for "Unmute site." Third, verify the correct output device is selected (speakers vs. headphones vs. Bluetooth). Fourth, some browsers require a user interaction on the page before allowing speech — try clicking anywhere on the page, then pressing Speak again. Fifth, if your browser shows "Speech not supported," update to a modern browser. Firefox, Chrome, Edge, and Safari all support SpeechSynthesis; Internet Explorer does not.

Can I change the speaking speed and pitch?

Yes — both are fully adjustable. The speed slider ranges from 0.5× (half speed — great for learning a new language or following complex text) to 2× (double speed — useful for listening to long articles quickly). The pitch slider ranges from 0 (very deep, almost robotic) to 2 (very high, cartoonish). The default for both is 1.0, which sounds natural. For audiobook-style listening, try speed 0.9–1.1 and pitch 1.0. For accessibility use, speed 1.2–1.5 and pitch 1.0 helps process information faster. For a friendly, warm narration, try pitch 0.9 with speed 1.0. Adjustments take effect the next time you click Speak.

Can I download the spoken audio as an MP3 file?

Not directly in the browser — this is a limitation of the SpeechSynthesis API, which plays audio in real-time but doesn't expose it as a downloadable file for security reasons. However, you can record the audio using two workarounds: (1) Use the DocEasy Screen Recorder tool with "tab audio" enabled, then extract the audio using the Extract Audio tool; (2) On Windows, enable "Stereo Mix" in your sound settings and use DocEasy's Voice Recorder to capture the output. Both methods produce an MP3 or WebM file that you can download and share. For professional TTS with downloadable output, dedicated services like ElevenLabs or Google Cloud TTS work better, but they aren't free or private.

Which languages are supported?

The Text to Speech tool supports any language your device has voices for — which is usually 30+ languages on modern devices. On Windows, the default set includes English (US, UK, Australia, India), Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Russian, Japanese, Chinese, Korean, and more. On macOS, Apple provides 40+ languages with high-quality voices. On Android, Google TTS supports 50+ languages. To see what's available, open the Voice dropdown — each entry shows the voice name and language code (like "en-US" for American English). If a language you need isn't listed, install additional language packs from your OS's speech settings.

Can I use Text to Speech for commercial projects?

Yes — anything you create using browser-based TTS is yours to use freely, including commercially. However, be aware that some operating system voices have subtle licensing terms — for example, Microsoft's voices on Windows are technically licensed for personal use on that specific machine. For YouTube videos, podcasts, or commercial products where you need full commercial rights, we recommend using Apple's voices (macOS/iOS), which are licensed more permissively, or dedicated TTS services with explicit commercial licenses. For personal projects, education, accessibility, or internal use, browser TTS is completely fine with no restrictions.

Does it work offline?

Yes — the Text to Speech tool works fully offline after the page loads once. Since voices are stored locally on your device and the SpeechSynthesis API processes text locally, no internet connection is needed to generate speech. This is useful for flights, areas with poor connectivity, or privacy-conscious usage where you don't want any network activity. Just load the page once while online, then you can disconnect Wi-Fi and continue using it. The only exception is if your device's voices are set to stream from a cloud service (rare, but possible with some Android setups) — in that case, offline mode may not work for those specific voices.

Why does the voice sound robotic or unnatural?

Voice quality depends entirely on your operating system and which voices are installed. Older SAPI voices on Windows (like "Microsoft David" or "Microsoft Zira") sound somewhat robotic because they were designed in the 2010s. Modern voices — like the newer Windows 11 "Natural" voices, Apple's "Siri" voices, or Google's high-quality voices — sound dramatically better, almost human. To improve quality: (1) Update your OS to get newer voices; (2) On Windows 11, install additional "Natural" voice packs from Settings → Time & Language → Speech; (3) On macOS, download "Enhanced" or "Premium" voices in System Preferences → Accessibility → Spoken Content; (4) On Android, install Google's high-quality TTS voices in Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-speech output.

Can I pause, resume, or stop mid-speech?

Yes — the tool supports all three controls. Click "Pause" while speech is playing, and it freezes mid-sentence. Click "Pause" again (now showing "Resume"), and it continues from exactly where it stopped. Click "Stop" at any time to end speech completely. Note that some browsers and voices may pause at a word boundary rather than mid-word — this is a browser limitation, not a bug. Also, on some devices, pause/resume works better on longer texts than very short ones. If pause doesn't seem to work, try pausing and resuming on a longer paragraph — that usually behaves more reliably than single sentences.

Is there a character limit?

The tool allows up to 5,000 characters per session, which is roughly 800–1,000 words — about 5 minutes of spoken audio. This limit is a browser constraint (longer texts may cause the SpeechSynthesis API to fail or stutter). If you need to read longer documents, simply split the text into sections, speak the first section, clear the box, paste the next, and continue. For reading entire books, we recommend dedicated audiobook tools, but for essays, articles, study notes, and short documents, 5,000 characters is more than enough. Character count is displayed live below the text box as you type.

How is this different from the Neural TTS tool?

The regular Text to Speech tool uses your operating system's built-in voices via the SpeechSynthesis API — fast, private, but limited to voices already installed on your device. The Neural TTS tool (available separately) uses AI models like Piper or Coqui that run in the browser via WebAssembly, generating more natural-sounding speech at the cost of a one-time model download (~20–50 MB). Use the regular tool for quick reads, offline use, and languages you already have voices for. Use Neural TTS for high-quality narration, voiceovers, or when your OS voices sound too robotic. Both are free and private; they just use different technologies.

Why do some words sound wrong or mispronounced?

TTS engines rely on pronunciation dictionaries and language rules, and they occasionally mispronounce uncommon words, proper nouns, technical jargon, or words that are spelled the same but pronounced differently (like "read" vs. "read"). To improve pronunciation: (1) Spell tricky words phonetically in the text (e.g. "Kubernetes" → "koo-ber-net-eez"); (2) Add commas and periods to influence pacing and intonation; (3) Use a voice in the language the text is written in — English voices will butcher French or German words; (4) For names, try writing them in a way that forces correct pronunciation. Some high-quality voices handle pronunciation better than others — experiment with different voices to find the one that reads your content most naturally.

Chrome and Edge on Windows offer the widest voice selection and smoothest playback. Safari on macOS and iOS provides Apple's high-quality voices. If you see "Speech not supported" on the Speak button, your browser is outdated or TTS is disabled — please update your browser to the latest version.

Can I use it to learn a new language?

Absolutely — Text to Speech is an excellent language-learning tool. Here's how to use it effectively: (1) Type a sentence in your target language (Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Japanese, etc.); (2) Select a native speaker voice for that language from the dropdown; (3) Slow the speed to 0.6×–0.8× to hear each word clearly; (4) Listen repeatedly, then try to repeat aloud yourself; (5) Gradually increase speed to 1.0× as you improve. This method works better than flashcards because you hear natural pronunciation and intonation. Pair it with DocEasy's Voice Recorder to record yourself mimicking the TTS voice, then compare — a fast way to improve accent.
