Whether you want to listen to an article while commuting, convert study notes into audio for revision, prepare voiceovers for a video, or make your content accessible to visually impaired users, DocEasy's free text to speech tool converts any written text into natural-sounding speech using your browser's built-in voices — no install, no signup, no watermark, and complete privacy because your text never leaves your device.

The DocEasy Text to Speech tool converts any text into speech using voices built into your device. Choose voice, speed, and pitch.

How to Use Text to Speech

Reading text aloud takes just four simple steps:

  1. Step 1 — Paste your text: Type or paste up to 5,000 characters (roughly 800–1,000 words) into the text box.
  2. Step 2 — Choose a voice: Open the "Voice" dropdown. It lists every voice installed on your device — usually 5–50 depending on your OS and language packs.
  3. Step 3 — Adjust speed and pitch (optional): Speed ranges from 0.5× (half) to 2× (double). Pitch ranges from 0 (deep) to 2 (high). Defaults are 1.0 for both.
  4. Step 4 — Click "Speak": The text is read aloud immediately. Click "Pause" to freeze mid-sentence, "Resume" to continue, or "Stop" to end completely.

That's it — no signup, no email, no payment. Everything runs locally in your browser tab.

Where Do the Voices Come From?

The tool uses the browser's built-in SpeechSynthesis API, which relies on voices installed on your operating system:

  • Windows 10/11: 5–15 voices including Microsoft David, Zira, Mark, and various language packs.
  • macOS / iOS: 20–50 high-quality voices including Samantha, Alex, Daniel, and multilingual options.
  • Android: Fewer built-in voices, but more can be installed via Google's TTS engine.
  • Chrome OS: 3–10 voices.
  • Linux: Varies by distribution and installed speech engines (eSpeak, Festival, Pico).

Because voices are stored on your device, the tool works fully offline after loading once. And because nothing is sent to any server, your text stays completely private.

When to Use Text to Speech

  • Listen to articles: Convert long reads into audio for commuting or exercising.
  • Study and revision: Turn notes, definitions, and formulas into audio you can repeat.
  • Proofread by ear: Hearing your own writing catches grammar mistakes your eyes miss.
  • Accessibility: Make content readable for visually impaired users or people with dyslexia.
  • Language learning: Hear native pronunciation of Spanish, French, German, Hindi, Japanese and more.
  • Voiceovers: Create draft audio for videos, presentations, or podcasts.
  • Audiobook-style narration: Convert chapters, essays, or documentation into audio.
  • Screen-free reading: Listen while cooking, driving, or doing chores.

Speed and Pitch — Best Settings

Different use cases call for different settings:

  • Audiobook listening: Speed 0.9–1.1×, Pitch 1.0 — natural, comfortable pacing.
  • Accessibility / quick reading: Speed 1.2–1.5×, Pitch 1.0 — process information faster.
  • Language learning: Speed 0.6–0.8×, Pitch 1.0 — hear each word clearly.
  • Warm narration: Speed 1.0×, Pitch 0.9 — friendly, welcoming tone.
  • Energetic delivery: Speed 1.1–1.2×, Pitch 1.1 — lively, engaging.

Adjustments take effect the next time you click Speak.

Improving Pronunciation

TTS engines occasionally mispronounce uncommon words, proper nouns, or technical jargon. To improve:

  • Spell phonetically: Write "Kubernetes" as "koo-ber-net-eez."
  • Add punctuation: Commas, periods, and dashes influence pacing and intonation.
  • Match language to text: Use an English voice for English text, Spanish for Spanish, etc.
  • Rewrite names: Try different spellings until the voice pronounces them correctly.

Privacy and Security

Text to speech is often used for sensitive content — legal documents, medical notes, private messages, or unreleased business plans. DocEasy is built so none of this ever leaves your device:

  • No uploads: Text is processed locally by your browser's SpeechSynthesis API.
  • No server storage: Nothing is saved anywhere except your device's temporary memory.
  • No tracking: We don't log what you type or which voice you choose.
  • Offline capable: After loading once, turn off Wi-Fi and continue reading aloud.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I use this Text to Speech tool?

Type or paste your text into the box, choose a voice from the dropdown, optionally adjust speed and pitch, then click "Speak." The browser reads the text aloud immediately. Pause, Resume, or Stop anytime.

Is this Text to Speech tool free?

Yes — 100% free, forever. No signup, no email, no payment, no character limit (within the 5,000-character session cap), and no premium tier. Every voice on your device is available at no cost.

Is my text sent to any server?

No. The tool uses your browser's built-in SpeechSynthesis API, which runs entirely offline. No text, no voice data, no metadata is transmitted. Verify by disconnecting Wi-Fi and clicking Speak — it still works.

How many voices are available?

The number depends on your device. Windows offers 5–15, macOS/iOS offers 20–50, Android offers a few (more via Google TTS), Chrome OS offers 3–10. Install additional language packs from OS settings for more voices.

Why can't I hear anything when I click Speak?

Check: (1) Device volume isn't muted; (2) Browser tab isn't muted (right-click tab → Unmute site); (3) Correct output device selected; (4) Click anywhere on the page first (some browsers require user interaction before speech).

Can I change the speaking speed and pitch?

Yes — the Speed slider ranges 0.5×–2× and Pitch ranges 0–2. Defaults are 1.0 for both. Adjustments take effect on the next Speak.

Can I download the spoken audio as MP3?

Not directly — this is a limitation of the SpeechSynthesis API, which plays audio in real-time but doesn't expose it as a file. Workarounds: use the Screen Recorder with tab audio, then extract audio. Or on Windows, enable Stereo Mix and record with the Voice Recorder.

Which languages are supported?

Any language your device has voices for — usually 30+ on modern devices. Open the Voice dropdown to see them all. If a language is missing, install additional language packs from OS settings.

Can I use Text to Speech for commercial projects?

Yes — anything you create using browser-based TTS is yours to use freely. Note that some OS voices have subtle licensing terms — check your OS documentation for details. For commercial content, macOS/iOS voices typically have more permissive terms.

Does it work offline?

Yes — after the page loads once, the tool works fully offline. Since voices are stored locally and processing is local, no internet is needed. Turn off Wi-Fi and continue using it.

Why does the voice sound robotic or unnatural?

Voice quality depends on your OS and installed voices. Older SAPI voices sound robotic. Modern voices (Windows 11 "Natural," Apple's Siri voices, Google's high-quality voices) sound almost human. Update your OS and install newer voice packs for better quality.

Can I pause, resume, or stop mid-speech?

Yes — click Pause to freeze mid-sentence, Resume to continue, Stop to end completely. Some browsers pause at a word boundary rather than mid-word — this is a browser limitation, not a bug.

Is there a character limit?

Yes — 5,000 characters per session (roughly 800–1,000 words, ~5 minutes of audio). For longer documents, split into sections and paste one at a time.

How is this different from the Neural TTS tool?

The regular Text to Speech uses your OS voices via SpeechSynthesis — fast, private, but limited to installed voices. Neural TTS uses AI models (Piper, Coqui) that generate more natural speech but require a one-time model download. Both are free and private; they just use different technologies.

Why do some words sound wrong or mispronounced?

TTS engines occasionally mispronounce uncommon words, proper nouns, or words with multiple pronunciations ("read" vs "read"). Fix by spelling tricky words phonetically, adding punctuation to influence pacing, or using a voice in the correct language.

Which browsers are supported?

Chrome 90+, Edge 90+, Firefox 88+, Safari 14+, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, and all Chromium browsers. Chrome and Edge on Windows offer the widest voice selection.

Do you keep logs of my text or voice?

No. We don't log what you type, which voice you choose, how long you speak, or any metadata. Our analytics only records anonymous page views. Since your text never leaves your browser, there's nothing to log.

Can I use it to learn a new language?

Absolutely — Text to Speech is excellent for language learning. Type a sentence in your target language, select a native-speaker voice, slow the speed to 0.6×–0.8×, listen repeatedly, then mimic. Pair with the Voice Recorder to compare your pronunciation.

What are the best uses for Text to Speech?

Listening to articles while commuting, converting study notes to audio, checking your own writing by ear, making content accessible for visually impaired users, preparing voiceovers, and improving foreign-language pronunciation.

Can I use it on mobile phones?

Yes — works on Android (Chrome, Firefox, Samsung Internet) and iOS (Safari 14+). Mobile voice selection is usually smaller than desktop, but Android users can install more voices via Google TTS settings.

Why does Safari sound different from Chrome?

Safari uses Apple's voice engine (macOS/iOS voices like Samantha, Alex), while Chrome on Windows uses Microsoft SAPI voices. Each OS has its own voice set, so the same text sounds different across platforms. This is expected behavior.

Can I export the audio to use in a video?

Not directly from the browser, but you can record the output: (1) Use the Screen Recorder with "System audio" or "tab audio" enabled; (2) Extract the audio using the Universal Media Editor; (3) Use the resulting MP3 in your video project.
