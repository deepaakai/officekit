How to Use the Mic Test

Testing your microphone takes just three clicks:

  1. Step 1 — Click "Start Test": Your browser will ask for microphone permission. Click "Allow" once — this is remembered for future visits.
  2. Step 2 — Speak into your microphone: You'll immediately see a green waveform that moves with your voice and a horizontal volume meter filling up from 0% to 100%.
  3. Step 3 — Read your device info: Below the meter, you'll see your microphone's label, sample rate, channel count, and a green "Working" status.

If the waveform moves and the meter reacts to your voice, your mic is working perfectly. If not, work through the troubleshooting checklist below.

What the Volume Meter Percentage Means

The percentage shows the loudness of your mic input in real-time, calculated using RMS (Root Mean Square) analysis — the same method used by professional audio meters:

  • 0–10% — Silence or very faint sound
  • 10–30% — Quiet, normal for distant speech
  • 30–60% — Ideal for recording (clear and strong)
  • 60–80% — Loud, good for close-mic recording
  • 80–95% — Very loud, risk of clipping distortion
  • 95–100% — Clipping — audio is distorting and quality is lost

For the best recordings, aim for peaks in the 50–75% range while speaking normally.

Common Reasons Your Mic Isn't Working

If the meter stays at 0% and the waveform is flat, work through these checks in order:

  1. Mic muted in OS: Windows — check the taskbar sound icon. macOS — check System Preferences → Sound → Input. Make sure input isn't muted or set to zero.
  2. Wrong device selected: If you have multiple mics (laptop built-in, headset, USB, webcam), your OS might be using the wrong one. Check your sound settings.
  3. Another app has the mic: Zoom, Teams, Discord, Skype, OBS, and browser tabs can all lock the microphone exclusively. Close them and refresh.
  4. Physical mute switch: Many headsets and USB mics have a hardware mute button or switch. Check it.
  5. Browser permission denied: Click the padlock icon in the address bar and set microphone to "Allow." Then refresh the page.

Privacy and Security

Your microphone input is never recorded, saved, or transmitted. The Mic Test uses the browser's Web Audio API to analyse audio in real-time and display the waveform and volume meter. No audio data is stored to disk, and no audio data leaves your device. The moment you click "Stop" or close the tab, everything is discarded.

You can verify this yourself: open Developer Tools (F12), go to the Network tab, click "Start Test," and speak. You'll see zero outgoing requests during the entire test.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I test my microphone with this tool?

Click "Start Test," grant microphone permission when your browser asks, and speak into your mic. You'll see a live waveform moving with your voice and a volume meter filling up. If both react, your mic works.

Is this microphone test free?

Yes — 100% free, forever. No signup, no email, no payment, no daily limit, and no premium tier. Test as many times as you want, on any device.

Is my voice recorded or sent to any server during the test?

No. The Mic Test analyses your mic input in real-time using the Web Audio API — nothing is stored or transmitted. You can verify by opening Developer Tools → Network tab — zero outgoing requests.

Why does the browser ask for microphone permission?

Every modern browser requires explicit permission before any website can access your microphone. This is a security feature to prevent malicious sites from secretly listening. Grant it once per browser and it's remembered.

My microphone shows 0% and no waveform — what's wrong?

Check: (1) Mic muted in OS; (2) Correct device selected; (3) Other apps (Zoom, Teams) not holding the mic; (4) Physical mute switch off; (5) Browser permission set to "Allow." Refresh the page after fixing.

Why does my microphone show a different label than expected?

For privacy, browsers sometimes display generic labels like "Default - Microphone (1)" if you've only granted temporary permission. Once you grant persistent permission, actual device names may appear.

What does the volume meter percentage mean?

It shows the loudness of your input via RMS analysis. 30–60% is ideal for recording, 60–80% is loud, 95–100% is clipping (distortion). Aim for peaks around 50–75%.

Does the test work on mobile phones?

Yes — works on Android (Chrome, Firefox) and iPhone/iPad (Safari 14.3+). Just tap "Start Test," grant mic permission, and speak.

Can I switch between multiple microphones?

Not directly in the tool. To test a different mic, click "Stop," change the default input device in your OS sound settings, then click "Start Test" again.

What sample rate and channels should I expect?

Most modern mics report 44,100 Hz (CD quality) or 48,000 Hz. Channel count is typically 1 (mono). Bluetooth headsets in call mode may show 8,000 or 16,000 Hz.

Why does my Bluetooth headset show low sample rate?

Bluetooth uses a lower-bandwidth profile (HFP) when the mic is active, reducing quality to 8–16 kHz. This is a Bluetooth limitation. Use a wired headset or USB mic for recording.

Can I test my microphone without speaking aloud?

Yes — gently tap the microphone body or the desk it rests on. Even a soft tap produces a visible spike on the waveform. A whisper should push the meter to 20–40%.

How is this different from recording a voice memo?

The Mic Test is a diagnostic tool — it shows live feedback but doesn't save anything. Use the Voice Recorder to record actual audio files that you can download and share.

Which browsers are supported?

Chrome 90+, Edge 90+, Firefox 88+, Safari 14+, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, and all Chromium browsers. Chrome and Edge offer the most detailed device information.

Can I use the mic test for job interviews or classes?

Absolutely — this is one of the most common uses. Run a 15-second mic test before any Zoom, Teams, or Meet call. Verify your mic is picking up 50–75% before speaking.

Does the test work offline?

Yes — after the page loads once, it works fully offline. Since analysis happens locally in your browser, you can turn off Wi-Fi and continue testing.

Why is my microphone picking up background noise?

Common causes: (1) High input gain — reduce OS input volume to 60–70%; (2) Omnidirectional mic — use a directional USB mic or headset mic; (3) No noise suppression — some apps (Zoom) apply filtering, but the Mic Test shows raw input.

Do you keep any logs of my microphone test?

No. We don't log device names, sample rates, or any data. Our analytics only records anonymous page views — never microphone activity. Since nothing leaves your browser, there's nothing to log.

What is RMS and why is it used?

RMS (Root Mean Square) is the standard way to measure audio signal level. It reflects perceived loudness more accurately than peak measurement. Professional audio meters use RMS for this reason.
