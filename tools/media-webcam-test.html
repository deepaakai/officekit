How to Use the Webcam Test

Testing your webcam takes just two clicks:

  1. Step 1 — Click "Start Camera": Your browser will ask for camera permission. Click "Allow" once — this is remembered for future visits.
  2. Step 2 — See your preview: The video feed appears immediately. Below it, you'll see four stats: resolution, frame rate, device label, and a green "Live" status.

If the video appears and stats show, your webcam is working perfectly. If not, work through the troubleshooting checklist below.

What the Stats Mean

  • Resolution: The pixel dimensions of your camera output. Common values: 640×480 (SD, rare), 1280×720 (HD, most common), 1920×1080 (Full HD), 2560×1440 (QHD), 3840×2160 (4K).
  • Frame Rate (FPS): The number of frames captured per second. 30 fps is standard; 60 fps is smoother. Lower FPS may indicate CPU strain or a bandwidth-limited camera.
  • Device Label: The name your OS gives the camera (e.g. "HD Webcam C920," "FaceTime HD Camera"). Some browsers show generic names for privacy.
  • Status: "✓ Live" confirms the camera stream is active and decoding properly.

Common Reasons Your Webcam Isn't Working

If the preview shows a black screen or nothing happens, work through these checks:

  1. Camera permission denied: Click the padlock icon in the address bar, set camera to "Allow," and refresh the page.
  2. Another app is using the camera: Zoom, Teams, Discord, Skype, OBS, and browser tabs can all lock the camera exclusively. Close them and try again.
  3. Physical privacy shutter: Many laptops have a slider that blocks the lens — check and open it.
  4. OS-level block: Windows — Settings → Privacy → Camera. macOS — System Preferences → Security & Privacy → Camera. Ensure your browser is allowed.
  5. Wrong device selected: If you have multiple cameras (built-in + USB), the browser uses the OS default. Change it in your OS sound/video settings.
  6. Driver issues: On Windows, open Device Manager and confirm the webcam isn't showing a warning icon. Update drivers if needed.

Privacy and Security

Your webcam feed is never recorded, saved, or transmitted. The Webcam Test only shows a live preview in your browser using the getUserMedia API. It does not record video, does not take screenshots, and does not send anything over the internet. The moment you click "Stop," the camera stream is fully terminated.

You can verify this yourself: open Developer Tools (F12), go to the Network tab, click "Start Camera," and watch. You'll see zero outgoing data — because the video stays entirely on your device.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I test my webcam with this tool?

Click "Start Camera," grant camera permission when the browser asks, and the live preview appears instantly. You'll see resolution, frame rate, device label, and a green "Live" status below the video.

Is this webcam test free?

Yes — 100% free, forever. No signup, no email, no payment, no daily limit, and no premium tier. Test as many times as you want, on any device.

Is my camera being recorded or sent anywhere?

No. The Webcam Test only shows a live preview. Nothing is recorded, saved, or transmitted. Verify by opening Developer Tools → Network tab — zero outgoing requests during the test.

Why does my browser ask for camera permission?

Every modern browser requires explicit permission before any website can access the camera. This prevents malicious sites from secretly spying on you. A "Stop Sharing" bar stays visible whenever the camera is active.

My webcam shows a black screen — what's wrong?

Check: (1) Camera permission set to "Allow" in the browser; (2) No other app (Zoom, Teams) holding the camera; (3) Physical privacy shutter open; (4) OS privacy settings allow browser access; (5) Correct camera selected in OS settings.

What resolution and frame rate should I expect?

Most laptop webcams output 1280×720 at 30 fps. External USB webcams (Logitech C920/C922) output 1920×1080 at 30 fps. Newer high-end webcams support 60 fps and 4K.

How can I check if my camera looks good before an interview?

Verify: (1) Resolution at least 720p; (2) Even lighting with light source in front (not behind); (3) Clean background; (4) Camera at eye level; (5) You look directly at the lens, not the preview.

Does the test work on mobile phones?

Yes — works on Android (Chrome, Firefox) and iPhone/iPad (Safari 14.3+). Tap "Start Camera," grant permission, and the front camera shows live. Mobile resolutions may be higher than desktop.

Can I switch between multiple cameras?

Not directly in the tool. To test a different camera, click "Stop," change the default camera in your OS settings, then click "Start Camera" again. Some browsers let you pick a specific device from the permission popup.

Why does my webcam label show as generic text?

For privacy, browsers sometimes show labels like "Camera (1)" until you grant persistent permission. Once granted, actual device names may appear. Corporate-managed devices may hide labels permanently.

Does it work offline?

Yes — after the page loads once, it works fully offline. Since the preview runs entirely locally via getUserMedia, no internet is needed. Turn off Wi-Fi and continue testing.

Which browsers are supported?

Chrome 90+, Edge 90+, Firefox 88+, Safari 14+, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, and all Chromium browsers. Chrome and Edge offer the fastest preview startup.

How do I fix a laggy or low-fps preview?

Close other CPU-heavy apps (Zoom, OBS, games), close other browser tabs with video, disable browser extensions temporarily, and check that Windows Privacy → Camera is enabled for your browser.

Can I take a screenshot of the webcam preview?

Yes — the tool has no built-in screenshot button, but use your OS tool (Windows: Win+Shift+S, macOS: Cmd+Shift+4). Or use the Screen Recorder with "Current Tab" to capture the preview as video.

Why does my webcam look blurry or out of focus?

Common causes: (1) Dirty lens — wipe with a microfibre cloth; (2) Autofocus hasn't locked — move the camera slightly; (3) Low resolution — verify 720p+ in the stats; (4) Software interference — close Zoom/Skype and refresh.

What if the stats show but the video is black?

This usually means DRM, driver issues, or GPU decode failure. Try switching to Chrome or Edge, updating your graphics driver, or restarting the browser. If a virtual camera app is running, close it and retry.
