Webcam Test — Frequently Asked Questions

Everything about testing your webcam — resolution, frame rate, troubleshooting, and privacy.

How do I test my webcam with this tool?

Testing your webcam takes just two clicks. First, click the green "Start Camera" button on this page. Second, when your browser asks for camera permission, click "Allow" — this is a one-time grant. Your webcam feed appears immediately in the preview box. Below the video, you'll see four pieces of information: your camera's resolution (like 1280 × 720), the frame rate (usually 30 or 60 fps), the device label (like "HD Webcam C920"), and a green "Live" status. If the video appears and the stats show, your webcam is working perfectly. To stop, click "Stop" — the camera stream terminates immediately and no data is retained.

Is my camera being recorded or sent anywhere?

No — nothing is recorded, saved, or transmitted. The Webcam Test only displays a live preview in your browser using the getUserMedia API. It does not record video, does not take screenshots, and does not send anything over the internet. The moment you click "Stop," the camera stream is fully terminated and nothing remains anywhere. You can verify this yourself: open Developer Tools (F12), go to the Network tab, and watch for requests while the camera runs. You'll see zero outgoing data — proving that everything stays on your device. This makes DocEasy's webcam test safe for checking cameras before job interviews, online classes, or sensitive video calls.

Why does my browser ask for camera permission?

Every modern browser requires explicit user permission before any website can access the camera. This is a fundamental security feature — it prevents malicious websites from secretly spying on you. When you click "Start Camera" for the first time, a popup appears asking "Allow this site to use your camera?" Click "Allow" to proceed. You only grant this permission once per browser — after that, it's remembered. If you accidentally clicked "Block," you can reset it by clicking the padlock or information icon next to the URL in your address bar, changing camera permission to "Allow," and refreshing the page. On mobile, similar permission prompts appear on first use.

My webcam shows a black screen — what's wrong?

A black screen usually means the camera is either blocked, in use by another app, or physically disabled. Work through these checks: (1) Verify camera permission is set to "Allow" in your browser — click the padlock icon in the address bar. (2) Close other apps that might be using the camera — Zoom, Teams, Discord, Skype, OBS, and other browser tabs with video tools all lock the camera exclusively. (3) Check for a physical privacy shutter on your laptop (many have a slider that blocks the lens) or a keyboard shortcut (like Fn+F8 on some Lenovo/HP laptops). (4) On Windows, check Settings → Privacy → Camera and confirm the toggle is on. (5) On macOS, check System Preferences → Security & Privacy → Camera. After fixing, refresh the page and try again.

What resolution and frame rate should I expect?

Most modern webcams output one of the following resolutions: 640 × 480 (SD, rare now), 1280 × 720 (HD, most common), 1920 × 1080 (Full HD, common on newer laptops and USB webcams), 2560 × 1440 (QHD, rare), or 3840 × 2160 (4K, only on high-end webcams). Frame rates are typically 30 fps (standard) or 60 fps (smoother, on better webcams). Laptop built-in cameras usually output 720p at 30 fps. External USB webcams like the Logitech C920/C922/C930e output 1080p at 30 fps. If your resolution shows lower than expected, check that no other app is limiting it — Zoom and Teams sometimes lock the camera to 720p. Close other apps and refresh the page to see the full resolution.

How can I check if my camera looks good before an interview?

Use the Webcam Test as a 2-minute pre-interview checklist. First, verify the resolution is at least 720p — anything lower will look blurry on video calls. Second, check your lighting — your face should be evenly lit, with the light source in front of you (a window or ring light), not behind you. Third, check the background — a plain wall or bookshelf looks professional; a messy room with a bed or laundry does not. Fourth, position the camera at eye level — looking down at the camera looks unprofessional. Fifth, look directly at the camera lens while speaking, not at your own preview. Once you've adjusted, click "Stop" and open your interview app with confidence.

Does the test work on mobile phones?

Yes — the Webcam Test works on Android phones, iPhones, and iPads running modern browsers. On Android, Chrome and Firefox support the getUserMedia API, so the test works instantly. On iPhone and iPad, Safari (iOS 14.3 and later) supports it too. Just tap "Start Camera," grant camera permission, and the front or rear camera shows live. On mobile, the resolution may be higher than desktop (some phones have 12 MP cameras) but browsers typically downsample to 1280 × 720 for privacy and performance. Front camera is usually selected by default; the tool cannot currently switch between front and rear on mobile, but you can change this in your browser's camera permission settings.

Can I switch between multiple cameras?

Not directly within this tool — it uses your operating system's default camera. To test a different camera (like an external USB webcam when you have a laptop built-in one), first click "Stop," then change the default camera in your OS settings (Windows: Settings → Bluetooth & Devices → Cameras; macOS: no default, but apps request specific devices), then click "Start Camera" again. Alternatively, some browsers let you pick a specific device from the permission popup dropdown. On desktop, external USB webcams usually appear automatically when plugged in — after plugging one in, refresh the page and click "Start Camera" to use it.

Why does my webcam label show as generic text?

For privacy reasons, browsers sometimes show generic labels like "Camera (1)" or "Integrated Camera" instead of the actual device name. This happens most often when you've granted temporary permission (just for this session) rather than persistent permission. Once you grant persistent camera access (which typically happens after you click "Allow" and then refresh, or use the site again), the browser may reveal the actual device name like "Logitech BRIO" or "FaceTime HD Camera." On corporate-managed devices, labels may remain hidden by IT policy — this is intentional and cannot be changed.

Since the camera preview runs entirely in your browser using the local getUserMedia API, no internet connection is required once the page is loaded. You can switch off Wi-Fi and still test your camera. This is useful in locations with poor connectivity or for privacy-conscious users who want to be 100% sure that no data is being transmitted. The only requirement is that the page has fully loaded once. If you need to verify offline functionality, turn off Wi-Fi, click "Start Camera," and confirm the preview still appears.

How do I fix a laggy or low-fps preview?

If your webcam preview is choppy, laggy, or shows a low frame rate, try these fixes. First, close other apps that might be competing for CPU — Zoom, Teams, OBS, and Discord are the usual culprits. Second, close other browser tabs with video content — YouTube, Twitch, Netflix all consume CPU and GPU resources. Third, disable browser extensions temporarily (especially ad blockers and video downloaders). Fourth, on Windows, change your camera settings in Privacy → Camera to "Let apps access your camera" if it's off. Fifth, check if your webcam supports 60 fps and whether your browser is requesting it — some cameras default to 30 fps and don't advertise 60 fps support. If the lag persists, the camera hardware itself may be the bottleneck.

Why does my webcam look blurry or out of focus?

Blurry webcam output has three common causes. First, the lens is dirty — fingerprints and dust are the #1 cause of blurry webcams. Gently wipe the lens with a microfibre cloth. Second, autofocus hasn't locked — some webcams take a few seconds to autofocus. Move the camera slightly or cover and uncover the lens to trigger refocus. Third, the resolution is too low — if the camera is outputting 480p and being displayed at a larger size, it will look pixelated. Check the resolution in our test's stats — anything below 720p will look soft on modern screens. Fourth, software interference — some apps (Zoom, Skype) apply aggressive denoise that softens the image. Close them and refresh the page to see the raw webcam output.

Can I use my phone as a webcam?

Yes — with the right app. You can use your phone as a webcam for your computer by installing apps like Iriun Webcam, DroidCam, Camo, or EpocCam. These apps create a virtual camera on your PC/Mac that appears in our Webcam Test as a selectable device. Since these apps typically have higher-quality cameras than laptop webcams, this is a popular upgrade for content creators. Once the virtual camera is installed and running, refresh the Webcam Test page, click "Start Camera," and select the virtual camera from the permission dropdown if prompted. The result: your phone's 4K or 108MP camera feeding your desktop video calls with professional quality.
