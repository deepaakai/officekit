1. Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) vs Lump Sum Returns

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) enables investors to allocate a fixed sum into mutual fund schemes at regular intervals (typically monthly). This approach leverages Rupee Cost Averaging: you acquire more fund units when market valuations dip and fewer units during market rallies, reducing the risks of timing the market.

Mathematical SIP Formula

M = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ - 1) / i] × (1 + i)

  • M: Final Maturity Corpus.
  • P: Monthly installment amount.
  • i: Periodic monthly rate of return ($i = \text{Annual Return} / 12 / 100$).
  • n: Total monthly contributions ($(\text{Years} \times 12) + \text{Months}$).

2. Fixed Deposits (FD) & Quarterly Compounding Standards

Bank Fixed Deposits remain a cornerstone for guaranteed capital preservation. In formal banking across India and international financial systems, FD interest compounds on a quarterly basis (4 cycles per year) rather than annually. Our calculator accurately models quarterly compounding to ensure your projected maturity perfectly aligns with official bank certificates.

3. Sovereign Retirement Systems (PPF, EPF & NPS)

  • Public Provident Fund (PPF): A 15-year sovereign savings vehicle under the EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax category. Interest compounds annually, with maximum annual deposits capped at ₹1,50,000.
  • Employee Provident Fund (EPF): Designed for salaried professionals. The employee contributes 12% of Basic + DA, while the employer contributes 3.67% to EPF (with the rest directed to EPS). Our tool factors in annual salary increments to project true retirement accumulations.
  • National Pension System (NPS): A market-linked pension scheme where savings accumulate tax-efficiently until retirement (age 60), after which a mandatory portion (minimum 40%) is converted into a monthly pension annuity.
