1. Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) vs Lump Sum

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) allows you to invest small, fixed sums periodically (usually monthly) into mutual funds. This strategy utilizes Rupee Cost Averaging: you purchase more fund units when market prices dip and fewer units when prices rise, reducing the stress of timing the market.

Mathematical SIP Formula

M = P × [((1 + i)ⁿ - 1) / i] × (1 + i)

  • M: Final Maturity Amount.
  • P: Monthly installment amount.
  • i: Monthly periodic rate of return ($i = \text{Annual Return} / 12 / 100$).
  • n: Total number of payments ($(\text{Years} \times 12) + \text{Months}$).

2. Fixed Deposits (FD) & Quarterly Compounding

Bank Fixed Deposits remain a popular choice for guaranteed capital preservation. Unlike simple annual interest, commercial banks compound FD interest on a quarterly basis (4 times a year). Our calculator accounts for this quarterly compounding structure so your projected maturity value aligns with bank figures.

3. Long-Term Retirement Schemes (PPF, EPF & NPS)

  • Public Provident Fund (PPF): A 15-year government scheme under the EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax category. Interest compounds annually, with maximum annual deposits capped at ₹1,50,000.
  • Employee Provident Fund (EPF): Designed for salaried professionals. The employee contributes 12% of Basic + DA, while the employer contributes 3.67% to EPF (with the rest going to EPS pension). Our calculator models annual salary hikes to provide an accurate retirement nest-egg estimate.
  • National Pension System (NPS): A cost-effective market-linked retirement vehicle where market gains accumulate until age 60, after which a mandatory portion (minimum 40%) is converted into a monthly pension annuity.
