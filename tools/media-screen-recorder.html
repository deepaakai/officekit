Complete Guide: Record Your Screen Online Free (2026)

Whether you're creating a software tutorial, recording an online class, capturing a Zoom meeting, or documenting a bug for your development team, a reliable screen recorder is essential. DocEasy's Screen Recorder gives you full-control screen capture in your browser — no install, no signup, no watermark, and complete privacy because nothing is ever uploaded.

DocEasy Screen Recorder captures your full screen, a single window, or a browser tab — with optional system audio and microphone.

What Can You Record?

The tool uses the browser's getDisplayMedia API to capture three types of content:

  • Entire Screen — Captures everything on your monitor, including desktop icons, open apps, and notifications.
  • Application Window — Captures only a single app window (like PowerPoint, Chrome, or a game). Other windows stay hidden.
  • Browser Tab — Captures only the content of one specific tab. Perfect for recording webinars, YouTube videos, or online classes. This also reliably captures tab audio on every OS.

System Audio vs Microphone

Two audio sources can be captured together or independently:

  • System audio — Whatever is playing through your speakers: YouTube, Zoom calls, music, or in-game sound. Works best on Chrome and Edge (Windows 10/11).
  • Microphone — Your voice, captured live. Ideal for narrating tutorials, recording commentary, or delivering a presentation.

Both can be recorded at the same time, which is useful for product demos where you want to explain what's on screen while also capturing video audio.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Step 1 — Choose audio sources: Tick "System audio" and/or "Microphone" based on what you want to capture.
  2. Step 2 — Click "Start Recording": Your browser shows a picker dialog.
  3. Step 3 — Select content: Choose Entire Screen, Window, or Browser Tab, then click "Share."
  4. Step 4 — Record: The timer counts up, and a "Stop Sharing" bar appears at the top of your screen.
  5. Step 5 — Pause if needed: Click "Pause" to temporarily stop; click again to resume.
  6. Step 6 — Stop: Click "Stop" here or "Stop Sharing" in the browser bar.
  7. Step 7 — Download: Preview the result and click "Download" to save the WebM file.

When to Use Each Recording Mode

Entire Screen

Use when the recording needs to show desktop icons, multiple apps, or file systems. Common scenarios: recording software installation, capturing OS-level bugs, showing desktop workflows.

Application Window

Use when you want a clean recording of a single app. Notifications and pop-ups from other apps stay hidden. Common scenarios: recording PowerPoint presentations, demos of a specific app, single-game capture.

Browser Tab

Use for any browser-based content. Most reliable for audio capture. Common scenarios: recording YouTube videos, webinars, online classes, Twitch streams, or Google Slides presentations.

Output Format and Quality

Recordings are saved as .webm files using VP9 video codec and Opus audio codec — both modern, efficient, and royalty-free. WebM plays natively in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, VLC, and most modern media players. Typical file size: 5–15 MB per minute for 1080p screen content. If you need MP4, use the DocEasy Universal Media Editor to convert WebM → MP4 in a few seconds.

Best Practices for High-Quality Screen Recordings

  • Close unnecessary apps: Frees CPU/GPU, produces smoother video.
  • Use a decent microphone: Even a basic headset mic beats your laptop's built-in mic.
  • Record in a quiet room: Turn off fans and AC. Soft furniture reduces echo.
  • Test first: Do a 15-second test run before recording the full session.
  • Use 1080p resolution: Higher resolutions eat more CPU and produce large files.
  • Plug in power: Screen recording drains battery quickly on laptops.
  • Close notifications: Windows "Do Not Disturb" or macOS "Focus" prevents pop-ups during recording.
  • Hide the cursor (or don't): For tutorials, keeping the cursor visible is helpful. For polished videos, hide it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this screen recorder really free?

Yes — 100% free, forever. No signup, no email, no watermark, no time limit, no file-size cap, and no premium tier. You can record as many sessions as you want, for as long as you want, without paying anything.

Is my screen uploaded to your servers?

No. Recording happens entirely in your browser using the getDisplayMedia API. The video stream never leaves your device. You can verify this by opening Developer Tools → Network tab — you'll see zero outgoing requests carrying your video data.

Can I record system audio?

Yes — tick "System audio" before starting. Works best on Chrome and Edge (Windows 10/11). On macOS, use Browser Tab recording for reliable audio capture.

Can I record with my microphone?

Yes — tick "Microphone." Your browser will ask for mic permission separately. Ideal for tutorials, narrations, and presentations.

What format is the recording saved in?

WebM (VP9 + Opus). Plays natively in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, VLC, and most media players. Convert to MP4 with DocEasy's Universal Media Editor if needed.

What's the maximum recording length?

No imposed limit. Recording length depends on your device's available memory. A one-hour 1080p recording is roughly 300–500 MB, which most modern devices handle fine.

Does it work on mobile phones?

Partially. Chrome on Android (version 88+) supports tab recording. Safari on iOS does not support getDisplayMedia yet. For full-device screen recording on mobile, use the built-in OS screen recorder.

Can I pause and resume mid-recording?

Yes — click "Pause" while recording. The timer stops and no content is captured. Click again to resume. Paused sections are removed from the final recording.

Why does the browser ask for screen-sharing permission?

Every modern browser requires explicit user permission before any website can capture your screen. This is a security feature — no website can silently record your screen. A "Stop Sharing" bar remains visible the entire time, so you always know when recording is active.

Does it work offline?

Yes — after loading once, you can turn off Wi-Fi and continue recording. Everything runs locally in your browser.

Can I record DRM-protected content (Netflix, Disney+)?

No. DRM-protected content will appear black in the recording. This is enforced by the content provider, not a limitation of DocEasy.

Can I use the recordings commercially?

Yes — everything you record is yours to use freely, including commercially. The only exception is if the content you record is itself copyrighted by someone else.
