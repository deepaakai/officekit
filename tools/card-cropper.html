What is ID Card Cropping?

ID card cropping is the process of digitally extracting the card's front and back sides from a scanned PDF, photo, or combined page, and exporting them at a specific physical size and print resolution. The purpose is to produce a clean, print-ready image of the card that can be uploaded to government portals, printed on plastic or paper, or stored in a digital locker.

Unlike simply taking a photograph, cropping ensures that the output is perfectly aligned, correctly sized, and rendered at the right DPI for its intended use. This is critical because almost every official portal — from Aadhaar's myAadhaar service to banks, insurance providers, and exam registration systems — has strict size and quality requirements that a raw photo will not meet.

Why 300 DPI Matters

DPI (Dots Per Inch) determines the physical print quality of an image. Government portals, exam authorities, and banks usually specify 200–300 DPI for uploaded ID cards so that text, QR codes, and photos remain legible. At 72 DPI, text becomes blurry and QR codes stop scanning; at 300 DPI, every detail is razor-sharp even when printed at full size.

This tool renders your cards at 300 DPI by internally using the original PDF's vector data at up to 4× display resolution — guaranteeing sharp output every time. If your source is a photograph, we upscale intelligently to preserve edges, reduce JPEG artifacts, and maintain legibility.

Single-Side vs Dual-Side Cropping

Some documents contain only one side of a card (for example, a scanned front only). The tool provides Front and Back toggles in the toolbar:

  • Turn off Back if your document only has the front side. Only the front will be exported and printed.
  • Turn off Front if your document only has the back side. Only the back will be exported and printed.
  • Keep both ON if you have both sides — the tool exports and prints two images (front and back) side by side.
  • At least one side must remain enabled at all times.

Many ID card PDFs (especially e-Aadhaar and PAN) are password-protected when downloaded. The password formats differ by document:

PAN Card

Pro Tips for Perfect ID Card Scans

  • Scan at 300 DPI: In your scanner settings, choose at least 300 DPI for the sharpest results.
  • Use a matte surface: Place the card on a matte dark surface in daylight to avoid glare and shadows.
  • Avoid camera flash: Use natural daylight instead of flash — flash creates hot spots that hide fine print.
  • Keep the card flat: A bent or curved card produces distorted text. Flatten it before scanning.
  • Verify before submitting: Zoom to 200% after cropping to verify the card number and QR code are readable.
  • Keep both sides: Even if the portal only asks for the front, save both sides for future needs.
  • Use PNG for masters: Save HD PNG images as your master copies. Convert to JPG only when a portal requires it.
