Complete Guide: Crop Aadhaar & PAN Cards at 300 DPI Online

Applying for a government exam, opening a bank account, submitting KYC documents to a fintech app, or verifying your identity on a job portal almost always demands the same thing — clean, correctly-sized copies of your Aadhaar card and PAN card, showing both the front and back. But scanned Aadhaar PDFs are password-protected, phone photos come out blurry, and manually cropping two cards to exact dimensions is a headache. The OfficeKit Aadhaar & PAN Dual-Side Cropper solves all of this in one browser-based tool that produces ultra HD 300 DPI output — without ever uploading your sensitive identity documents to any server.

Dual-side crop boxes over a scanned Aadhaar card, exporting at exactly 8.5 × 5.5 cm (CR80) in 300 DPI print resolution.

Why 300 DPI Matters for Aadhaar & PAN Submissions

DPI stands for Dots Per Inch and determines the physical print quality of an image. Most government portals, exam authorities (UPSC, SSC, IBPS), and banks specify that uploaded ID card images must be at least 200–300 DPI to ensure all text, QR codes, and photo details remain legible. A phone photo taken at 72 DPI looks fine on a screen but produces blurry, unreadable text when printed or zoomed by a verifying officer. This tool renders your Aadhaar and PAN cards at 300 DPI by internally using the original PDF's vector data at up to 4× the display resolution — guaranteeing razor-sharp text, barcodes, and photographs every time.

Traditional online cropping tools downsample your PDF to a screen-sized bitmap before you crop, permanently losing pixel data. Even if you zoom in, the text gets blurry because the underlying information is gone. OfficeKit takes a completely different approach — it keeps a hidden ultra-HD master canvas at roughly 2480 pixels wide (the equivalent of A4 at 300 DPI) and extracts your crop from that master, not from the compressed on-screen preview.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  • Step 1 — Upload your document: Click the upload zone or drag-and-drop your e-Aadhaar PDF, scanned JPG, or phone photo of your Aadhaar / PAN card. Both PDF and image formats are supported.
  • Step 2 — Enter the e-Aadhaar password (if prompted): Downloaded e-Aadhaar PDFs are password-protected using the format "first 4 letters of your name in CAPITALS + birth year" (e.g., for a person named Deepak born in 1998, the password would be DEEPAK1998 — actually it's DEEP1998, the first 4 letters). Enter it to unlock the PDF. Everything is decrypted locally in your browser.
  • Step 3 — Position the two crop boxes: Two draggable, resizable crop boxes appear automatically. The green box is for the front side (marked "Front") and the blue box is for the back side (marked "Back"). Drag each box to sit precisely over the corresponding card on the scanned page.
  • Step 4 — Adjust the size (optional): The default target size is 8.5 × 5.5 cm, which matches the international CR80 standard (the size of a credit card, Aadhaar, PAN, driving licence, and voter ID). If your specific form asks for a different size, enter it in the "Target Size" fields.
  • Step 5 — Fine-tune the crop boundaries: Use the 8 directional handles around each box — four corners for proportional resizing and four edges for single-axis adjustment — to align the box edges exactly with the card's physical boundaries. You can also drag the box from its center to move it without resizing.
  • Step 6 — Multi-page PDFs: If your PDF has multiple pages (like a merged e-Aadhaar with front and back on different pages), use the Prev / Next page navigation in the toolbar to switch pages and crop each side separately.
  • Step 7 — Download or print: Click "Save HD Images" to download two 1004 × 650 pixel PNG files (front and back at 300 DPI), or click "Print Ultra HD" to open your system print dialog with both cards laid out side-by-side on an A4 sheet at their true 8.5 × 5.5 cm size.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: Is my Aadhaar / PAN data safe with this tool?
Absolutely. OfficeKit processes everything in your browser. Your files are never uploaded, never stored, and never seen by anyone — including us. You can verify this by disconnecting your internet after the page loads; the tool will still work completely.

Q: What is the exact size of an Aadhaar / PAN card?
All Indian identity cards (Aadhaar, PAN, driving licence, voter ID) follow the CR80 standard: 85.6 mm × 54 mm, which is approximately 8.5 × 5.5 cm. This tool uses that size by default.

Q: How do I know my e-Aadhaar password?
The standard e-Aadhaar password format is the first four letters of your name in CAPITAL letters followed by your year of birth. For example, if your name is "Deepak Kumar" and you were born in 1998, the password is DEEP1998. This password is set by UIDAI, not by us — we cannot recover or reset it.

Q: Can I crop PAN cards using the same tool?
Yes. PAN cards are the same size as Aadhaar (CR80). The tool is called a "dual-side cropper" precisely because it works with both Aadhaar and PAN, and any other government ID in the same format.

Q: Will the downloaded image be blurry when printed?
No. The output is rendered at 300 DPI from the original PDF's vector data, which is the industry standard for professional print. You can print at 100% size and the text will be razor-sharp.

Q: Can I use this on a mobile phone?
Yes. The interface is fully responsive. On mobile, tap and drag the crop boxes with your finger, and use the corner handles to resize. The HD rendering works identically on mobile and desktop.

Q: What if my scanned Aadhaar is tilted or rotated?
The crop boxes are axis-aligned rectangles, so they work best with upright documents. If your scan is tilted, re-scan it upright first, or rotate the image in a basic photo editor before uploading. Perfectly upright scans give the best results.

Q: Is there any file size limit?
No fixed limit. Very large PDFs (50+ pages) may take a few seconds to load and render on older devices, but the tool will handle them without crashing.

Q: Can I crop more than two cards at once?
Currently the tool provides two crop boxes (front + back). If you have multiple cards on a single page (for example, family members' Aadhaar cards), you can process them in multiple sessions — crop two at a time, download, then re-upload for the next pair.

Pro Tips for Best Results

  • Scan Aadhaar and PAN at 300 DPI in your scanner's settings before uploading, if possible. It costs the same file size and gives the sharpest output.
  • If using a phone camera, place the card on a dark matte surface in bright indirect daylight to avoid glare and shadows.
  • Always place the green box on the front and the blue box on the back to keep your downloads organized.
  • After cropping, open the downloaded PNG in an image viewer and zoom in to 200% to verify that the card number, QR code, and photo are all clearly readable before submitting.
  • Keep a master copy of your cropped PNGs in a secure folder so you never need to re-crop for future applications.
