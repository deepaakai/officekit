What Makes a Compliant Passport Photo?

Every government portal — from Indian Passport Seva to SSC, Railway RRB, UPSC, IBPS, NEET, JEE, and even international visa applications — has strict rules for the photo you upload. Understanding these rules saves you from repeated rejections. The six universal requirements are:

  • Correct size: 35 × 45 mm for Indian passports, 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm) for US visas, 35 × 45 mm for most Schengen visas and exams.
  • Plain background: Blue, white, or light gray. No shadows, no textures, no patterns.
  • Face coverage: Head should occupy 70–80% of the photo's vertical height.
  • Neutral expression: Closed mouth, eyes open, no smiling.
  • No accessories: Remove glasses if possible, no hats, no earrings that cover facial features.
  • Recent photo: Taken within the last 6 months.

DocEasy's Head Guide overlay helps you position the face perfectly within these rules. Enable it in the Background tab to see the head and chin guidelines while adjusting.

Why 300 DPI Output Matters

DPI (Dots Per Inch) determines the physical print quality of your photo. Most government portals specify 300 DPI minimum. At 72 DPI (typical phone screenshot), text on ID cards and fine facial details blur when printed. At 300 DPI, every detail remains razor-sharp even at full size.

DocEasy renders every download — individual photo or full print sheet — at exactly 300 DPI. The JPEG file we generate has the DPI metadata embedded, so printers and Photoshop automatically recognize the correct physical size.

Transparent Background — When and Why

The transparent background option is one of the most requested features for:

Government Exam Photo Size Guide (2026)

Different exams and portals require slightly different sizes. Here's a complete reference:

  • SSC CGL / CHSL / GD: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 50 KB max, JPEG format
  • Railway RRB NTPC / Group D: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 50 KB max
  • UPSC IAS / CDS / NDA: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 300 KB max
  • NEET UG: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 200 KB max
  • JEE Main / Advanced: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 200 KB max
  • IBPS PO / Clerk: 4.5 × 3.5 cm (landscape), 50 KB max
  • CTET / State PSC: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 50–100 KB
  • Police / Army / AFCAT: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 20–50 KB
  • Indian Passport: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 10–1000 KB
  • PAN Card: 2.5 × 3.5 cm, 20 KB max
  • Driving Licence: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 20 KB max
  • US Visa / Green Card: 5.1 × 5.1 cm (2 × 2 inch), 240 KB max
  • Schengen Visa: 3.5 × 4.5 cm

Traditional photo printing wastes glossy paper because photos are placed with large margins or centered awkwardly. DocEasy's smart layout engine solves this:

  • Photos per Row: Choose how many photos you want across the sheet (2–8 or Auto). This lets you pack up to 8 photos in a single A4 row for maximum paper savings.
  • Total Photos: Pick exactly how many photos you need — 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, or Auto. Photos print from top-left to top-right, then next row down.
  • Sheet Margin: Adjust from 2mm (tight) to 20mm (spacious) to control how close photos are to the paper edge.

  • Auto-scaling: If you request more photos per row than naturally fit, the tool automatically scales them down slightly while preserving the exact mm dimensions on the final print.

Tips for the Perfect Passport Photo

  • Face the light: Stand facing a window so light hits your face evenly. No shadows under the eyes or nose.
  • Plain wall behind you: Stand 1–2 feet in front of a plain wall. Even if the AI removes it, a clean background helps the AI get sharp edges.
  • Hair off the face: Tuck hair behind ears and away from eyes. Fringe should not cover eyebrows.
  • Neutral expression: No smile. Eyes open, mouth closed.
  • Camera at eye level: Hold the phone at eye level, not above or below.
  • Vertical frame: Keep the photo portrait-oriented. The tool expects vertical for most presets.
  • Take multiple shots: Try 4–5 shots with slightly different angles and pick the one with the sharpest eyes.
  • Preview before print: Always zoom in after cropping to verify the face is well-lit and details are sharp.
