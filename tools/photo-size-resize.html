Complete Guide to Government Photo & Signature Sizes in India (2026)

If you've ever tried to upload a photo on a government portal — Parivahan, NSDL, SSC, UPSC, NTA, or the Passport Seva site — you've probably hit the dreaded error: "Invalid dimensions" or "File size exceeds limit." Every government form in India has strict requirements for photo dimensions, file size, and format. Getting any of these wrong means rejection — and re-application.

1. Why Government Photo Sizes Are So Strict

Government portals use automated validation systems. When you upload a photo, the server immediately checks:

  • Pixel dimensions — must match exactly (e.g., 350×350 for UPSC)
  • File size — must be between minimum and maximum KB (e.g., 10–20 KB for Driving Licence)
  • File format — usually JPG or JPEG, occasionally PNG
  • Aspect ratio — width-to-height ratio (e.g., 3.5:4.5 for passport photos)
  • Background — plain white or light only
  • Face position — must be centered, covering ~75% of the frame

Even a 1-pixel difference or 1-KB excess causes rejection. That's why millions of applications get rejected each year in India — not because of wrong information, but because of a wrong-sized photo.

The Sarathi Parivahan portal is India's centralised system for driving licence applications — learner's licence, permanent DL, and renewals. It has very specific photo requirements:

3. PAN Card Photo Size (NSDL / UTI)

PAN card applications and corrections — whether through NSDL (Protean) or UTIITSL — require a photo that fits into a small physical box on the form. The requirements:

DocumentDimensionsFile Size
Photo25 × 35 mm (2.5 × 3.5 cm)20–50 KB
Signature20 × 10 mm (2 × 1 cm)10–20 KB

The most common mistake here is uploading an Aadhaar-style photo (35×45 mm) instead of the smaller PAN-specific size. The form expects a small photo — larger ones get rejected.

4. Indian Passport Photo Size

For an Indian passport — whether a fresh application, reissue, or renewal — the Ministry of External Affairs specifies:

  • Size: 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)
  • File size: 10–100 KB (online portal)
  • Background: Plain white only
  • Face coverage: 70–80% of frame
  • Expression: Neutral, no smile
  • Glasses: Allowed only if not reflective; no tinted lenses
  • Head cover: Allowed only for religious reasons

Physical passport photos printed on paper for offline submission are 35 × 35 mm — but the online Passport Seva portal accepts 2×2 inch square photos.

5. US Visa Photo Size (DS-160)

The US Department of State's DS-160 visa application has one of the strictest photo specifications in the world:

  • Size: 2 × 2 inches (51 × 51 mm)
  • Pixels: 600 × 600 minimum, 1200 × 1200 maximum
  • File size: Under 240 KB
  • Format: JPEG only
  • Background: Plain white or off-white
  • Head height: 50–69% of total image height
  • Eye height: Between 56% and 69% from bottom of image

The US visa photo is square, not rectangular. Most Indian applicants submit a rectangular photo and get rejected instantly. Also, phones with portrait mode produce photos with blurry backgrounds — those are rejected too.

6. Aadhaar Photo Size

Aadhaar enrollment and updates (at an Aadhaar Seva Kendra) capture the photo directly via biometric devices. But for online services like self-service portal updates or downloading a new PVC Aadhaar:

  • Photo: 3.5 × 4.5 cm (for reference uploads)
  • File size: Up to 200 KB
  • Signature: 3.5 × 1.5 cm, up to 100 KB

On the UIDAI self-service update portal (SSUP), you can update address and other details online — but biometrics (including photo) still require a visit to an Aadhaar Seva Kendra.

7. Voter ID Photo Size (NVSP)

The National Voter's Service Portal requires:

  • Photo: 3.5 × 4.5 cm
  • File size: 20–100 KB
  • Format: JPG only
  • Signature: Not required separately

9. UPSC Exam Photo Sizes (IAS, NDA, CDS, CAPF)

UPSC's online application portal has one of the most modern photo requirements:

  • Photo: 350 × 350 pixels (square)
  • File size: 20–300 KB
  • Format: JPG/JPEG
  • Background: White or light

Unlike most exams, UPSC uses square photos (1:1 aspect ratio), not rectangular. If you upload a rectangular photo, the system rejects it.

10. NEET & Medical Exam Photo Sizes

NEET UG, NEET PG, NEET MDS, and AIIMS PG all follow NTA's photo requirements:

  • Photo: 3.5 × 4.5 cm (413 × 531 pixels at 300 DPI)
  • File size: 10–200 KB
  • Signature: 3.5 × 1.5 cm, 10–100 KB
  • Format: JPG/JPEG
  • Background: White only

NEET requires both photo and signature, and the signature must be on white paper with black or blue ink. The photo must have 80% face coverage with both ears visible.

11. JEE Main & JEE Advanced Photo Sizes

JEE Main (NTA-conducted) requires:

  • Photo: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 10–200 KB
  • Signature: 3.5 × 1.5 cm, 4–30 KB

JEE Advanced has similar requirements, though some IITs set slightly different limits. Always check the official information brochure for the exact year.

12. Banking Exam Photo Sizes (IBPS, SBI, RBI)

Banking exams have photo requirements that differ from SSC:

  • IBPS PO/Clerk: 4.5 × 3.5 cm (rectangular, landscape orientation), 20–50 KB
  • SBI PO/Clerk: 4.5 × 3.5 cm, 20–50 KB
  • RBI Grade B: 4.5 × 3.5 cm, 20–100 KB

Note the landscape orientation — width is greater than height. This is opposite to most exams, which use portrait orientation.

13. Railway Exam Photo Sizes (RRB, RPF)

  • RRB NTPC: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 20–50 KB
  • RRB Group D: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 20–50 KB
  • RRB ALP: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 20–50 KB
  • RPF Constable/SI: 35 × 45 mm, 20–70 KB

  • KVS/NVS TGT/PGT: 3.5 × 4.5 cm, 20–50 KB
  • DSSSB: 5 × 7 inch (large photo sheet), up to 300 KB

17. Why 300 DPI Matters for Government Photos

DPI (Dots Per Inch) determines print quality. Government portals specify photos in two ways:

  • Physical dimensions: e.g., 35 × 45 mm — for printing onto ID cards
  • Pixel dimensions: e.g., 350 × 350 px — for digital submission only

When a photo is specified in mm or cm, the DPI determines the actual pixel count. At 300 DPI (the government standard):

  • 35 mm × 45 mm = 413 × 531 pixels
  • 3.5 cm × 4.5 cm = 413 × 531 pixels
  • 2 × 2 inches = 600 × 600 pixels
  • 2.5 × 3.5 cm = 295 × 413 pixels

18. Common Photo Rejection Reasons

Based on data from thousands of rejected applications, these are the top reasons:

  • File size too large: Photo is 200 KB when the limit is 20 KB
  • File size too small: Photo is 5 KB, but the minimum is 10 KB
  • Wrong aspect ratio: Photo is square, but 3:4 is required
  • Wrong orientation: Photo is portrait, but landscape required (banking exams)
  • Wrong pixel dimensions: Photo is 400×500 instead of 350×350
  • Colored background: Blue or green background instead of white
  • Blurry or pixelated: Low-resolution photo from an old phone
  • Face too small or too large: Face coverage not in specified range
  • Red-eye or flash reflection: Photo taken with direct flash
  • Dark shadows: Photo taken in insufficient light
  • Photo not recent: More than 6 months old
  • No date printed: SSC requires date on photo since 2022

19. How to Take a Government-Compliant Photo at Home

You don't need a studio. Here's how to take a compliant photo with your phone:

  1. Stand in front of a plain white wall — a bedsheet or chart paper works fine.
  2. Use natural daylight — face a window around 10 AM or 4 PM. Avoid direct sunlight.
  3. No flash — flash creates shadows and red-eye.
  4. Hold the phone at eye level — not above (looking up) or below (looking down).
  5. Keep 3 feet distance — too close causes distortion; too far makes face small.
  6. Neutral expression — no smile, no raised eyebrows.
  7. No glasses, no cap, no hat — unless religious reasons apply.
  8. Both ears visible — hair should not cover ears.
  9. Wear formal or plain clothes — avoid busy patterns.
  10. Take 5–10 shots — pick the sharpest, best-lit one.
