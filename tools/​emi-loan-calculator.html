1. Formal Bank EMI: Reducing Balance Amortization

In formal banking (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Bank of America, Chase, Barclays), retail loans use the Reducing Balance Method. In this system, each Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) consists of two distinct parts: interest charges and principal recovery.

At the beginning of your tenure, the outstanding principal is high, so the interest component dominates each monthly payment. As your principal is gradually paid down, subsequent interest is computed solely on the smaller remaining balance, shifting future EMIs toward repaying the principal.

Mathematical Reducing Balance Formula

The standard formula used globally across banking engines is:

EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)ⁿ] / [(1 + r)ⁿ - 1]

  • P (Principal): Total loan amount sanctioned.
  • r (Monthly Interest Rate): Annual interest rate divided by 12 and then divided by 100 ($r = \text{Annual Rate} / 1200$).
  • n (Tenure in Months): Total number of monthly installments ($(\text{Years} \times 12) + \text{Months}$).

2. Private Loans & Local Monthly Interest ("ब्याज / सूद")

Informal financing, private financiers, agricultural money lenders, and peer-to-peer credit across India and many developing economies operate on monthly simple interest terms rather than annual reducing balances. In local parlance, these are expressed in percentage per month or "रुपये सैकड़ा" (rupees per hundred per month):

  • 1% Per Month (1 रुपया सैकड़ा): Equivalent to 12% annual simple interest.
  • 2% Per Month (2 रुपये सैकड़ा): Equivalent to 24% annual simple interest. A common rate for collateral-backed private credit.
  • 3% to 5% Per Month (3 से 5 रुपये सैकड़ा): Unsecured, high-risk micro-loans or urgent cash borrowing, compounding rapidly over long tenures.

In this structure, the monthly interest payment remains fixed throughout the loan duration unless an explicit partial principal repayment is made. Our Private Loan calculator shows both the exact monthly interest payout and the total cumulative liability.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How do I calculate an EMI for an odd period, such as 18 or 30 months?

Simply use the dual tenure inputs. To calculate for 18 months, enter 1 in the Years field and 6 in the Months field (or set Years to 0 and Months to 18). The calculator dynamically aggregates the full duration into the exact number of monthly cycles.

What is the difference between Flat Rate and Reducing Balance EMI?

In a flat rate loan, interest is calculated on the entire initial principal across the full tenure, meaning you pay interest on money you have already repaid. In a reducing balance loan, interest is recalculated each month only on the remaining balance, resulting in a substantially lower effective interest cost.

What does "₹2 सैकड़ा" (2 Rupees Saikda) mean in private loans?

In India, "2 रुपये सैकड़ा" denotes an interest rate of ₹2 per ₹100 per month, which equals 2% per month. Annually, this works out to a flat rate of 24% simple interest per year.

Can I download the amortization table for printing?

Yes. Click the green Export CSV Schedule button above the table. It will download an Excel-compatible spreadsheet showing your complete payment timeline, interest share, principal repaid, and ending balance.

Does this tool work for international currencies outside India?

Yes. Click the $ USD button in the top-right corner. The system updates all currency formatting, thousands separators, and CSV outputs to standard international denominations (Millions and Billions).
