Complete Guide: BMI, BMR, TDEE, and Asian vs WHO Standards

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a widely used screening tool. However, BMI alone does not tell the whole story. This calculator combines BMI with body fat estimate, ideal weight, BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate), and TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) for a complete health picture.

Why is BMI the same for men and women?

The BMI formula — Weight (kg) ÷ Height (m)² — contains no gender variable. Both WHO and ICMR (India) use identical cutoffs for both genders. However, men and women carry different body fat %, BMR and TDEE at the same BMI. That is why we show those gender-specific metrics separately.

Body Fat % (Deurenberg formula)

Body Fat % = 1.20 × BMI + 0.23 × Age − 10.8 × Gender − 5.4, where Gender = 1 for males, 0 for females. Women naturally have higher healthy body fat ranges than men.

Ideal Weight (Devine, 1974)

  • Men: 50 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 feet
  • Women: 45.5 kg + 2.3 kg per inch over 5 feet

BMR — Basal Metabolic Rate (Mifflin-St Jeor)

BMR is the number of calories your body burns completely at rest — for breathing, circulation, cell production, etc.

  • Men: BMR = 10×kg + 6.25×cm − 5×age + 5
  • Women: BMR = 10×kg + 6.25×cm − 5×age − 161

The 166 kcal gender gap you see for 70 kg / 170 cm / 25 y is because women naturally have less muscle mass and more fat mass.

TDEE — Total Daily Energy Expenditure

TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier. This is roughly how many calories you burn each day, and the number you should target for weight loss, maintenance or gain:

Activity LevelMultiplier
Sedentary (desk job, no exercise)1.2
Light (1–3 days/week)1.375
Moderate (3–5 days/week)1.55
Active (6–7 days/week)1.725
Very Active (hard daily training)1.9

Weight loss: eat ~500 kcal below TDEE. Weight gain: eat ~500 kcal above TDEE. Maintain: eat at TDEE.

Asian / Indian BMI Standard

Asians tend to have a higher body fat percentage at the same BMI compared to Westerners. Hence ICMR/WHO use lower cutoffs:

Frequently Asked Questions

Does BMI differ for men and women?

No. The BMI formula and cutoffs are identical for both genders. Gender affects body fat %, ideal weight, BMR, and TDEE — not the BMI number.

What is a good BMR?

BMR is not "good" or "bad" — it depends on your age, gender, weight, and height. Men typically have 150–300 kcal higher BMR than women of the same size. A higher BMR means you burn more calories at rest, which is usually beneficial for weight management.

Should I eat at TDEE to maintain weight?

Yes. Eating at your TDEE maintains current weight. Eating ~500 kcal below loses ~0.5 kg/week. Eating ~500 kcal above gains weight. These are estimates — adjust based on real-world results over 2–3 weeks.

Is a BMI of 24 good for an Indian?

No. Under the Asian/Indian standard, BMI 24 is "Overweight." The ideal range is 18.5 to 22.9.
