Voice Recorder — Frequently Asked Questions

Everything about recording audio in your browser — quality, formats, troubleshooting, and privacy.

How do I record my voice using DocEasy Voice Recorder?

Recording is simple and takes just three steps. First, click the pink "Start Recording" button on this page. Second, when your browser asks for microphone permission, click "Allow" — this is a one-time step. Third, speak into your microphone. You'll see the timer counting up, a red dot pulsing, and a live waveform bouncing with your voice. When you're done, click "Stop." A preview player appears below with your recording, and you can click "Download" to save it to your device. The whole process takes just a few seconds and requires zero installation, no account, and no email.

Yes — 100% free, forever, with no strings attached. There's no signup, no email required, no watermark, no daily limit, no file-size cap, and no "pro" version hiding behind a paywall. You can record as many voice memos as you want, as many times as you want, without ever paying a rupee. The site is supported by simple display ads on other pages, which is why we can keep this tool completely free while still protecting your privacy. We don't sell your data, we don't track your recordings, and we don't ask for your email to unlock features.

Where is my recording saved — on your server or my device?

Your recording is saved only on your device, never on any server. This is the most important thing to understand about DocEasy. When you click "Start Recording," the browser's MediaRecorder API captures audio directly into your device's memory. When you click "Download," the file is written to your Downloads folder. At no point does any data travel over the internet to DocEasy's servers — because we don't have any servers handling your audio. You can verify this yourself: disconnect your Wi-Fi after the page loads, and the recorder still works perfectly. This makes DocEasy the safest choice for recording sensitive content like client calls, medical notes, or private conversations.

Which audio format does the recording save in?

The format depends on your browser. Chrome, Edge, Brave, and Opera save recordings as .webm using the Opus codec at 128 kbps — excellent quality for voice. Safari on macOS and iOS saves as .m4a using AAC encoding, which is also high quality. Firefox saves as .ogg using Opus. All three formats play natively on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS without any extra software. If you specifically need MP3 — for example, to upload to a podcast host, WhatsApp, or a website — use DocEasy's Audio Converter tool to convert the downloaded file in a few seconds.

Does it work on mobile phones?

Yes — the Voice Recorder works perfectly on Android phones and iPhones running modern browsers. On Android, Chrome, Firefox, and Samsung Internet all support microphone recording out of the box. On iPhone, Safari (iOS 14.3 and later) supports MediaRecorder for audio, and Chrome for iOS also works. Just tap "Start Recording," grant microphone permission, and recording begins. The recording saves to your phone's Downloads folder. For the cleanest audio, use headphones with a built-in mic to reduce echo, and record in a quiet room. Mobile devices can also produce excellent recordings — many podcasters record entire episodes on their phones using this exact approach.

Why does my browser ask for microphone permission?

Every modern browser requires explicit permission before any website can access your microphone. This is a security feature — it prevents malicious sites from secretly listening to you. When you click "Start Recording" for the first time, you'll see a popup asking something like "Allow this site to use your microphone?" You must click "Allow" for the recorder to work. You only need to grant this permission once per browser — after that, it's remembered. If you accidentally clicked "Block," you can reset it by clicking the padlock icon in your browser's address bar and changing the microphone permission to "Allow."

Can I pause and resume mid-recording?

Yes — the Voice Recorder supports pausing and resuming without creating multiple files. Click "Pause" while recording, and the timer stops along with the audio capture. The red dot turns grey. Click "Resume," and recording continues from exactly where it stopped. When you finally click "Stop" and download, you get one continuous file with the paused sections removed automatically. This is perfect for recording interviews with breaks, or when you need to cough or sneeze without ruining the take. The pause/resume feature works reliably on Chrome, Edge, and Firefox; Safari support is also solid on iOS 15 and later.

How can I get the best audio quality?

Three tips will dramatically improve your recording. First, use an external microphone — even a ₹500 lavalier mic or a USB headset mic is far better than your laptop's built-in mic. Second, record in a small, soft-furnished room with curtains, carpet, or bookshelves to absorb echoes; avoid bathrooms, kitchens, and empty halls. Third, watch the waveform while recording — the green bars should stay in the middle range, never touching the top. If the bars are hitting the top constantly, you're too close to the mic or speaking too loudly; back off a few inches. You can also test your mic levels first using the Mic Test tool in the Device Tests section.

Is there a time limit on recordings?

There's no time limit imposed by DocEasy — you can record for as long as you want. The practical limit is memory. A 1-hour recording at 128 kbps is about 60 MB, which every device handles easily. A 5-hour recording is about 300 MB, which is still fine on desktop but might strain older phones. If you're recording for many hours continuously, we recommend plugging in your device, closing other apps, and keeping the browser tab in focus. There's also no limit on the number of separate recordings you can make — record 10, 100, or 1,000 times, it's all free.

Can I record audio playing on my computer (system audio)?

Not with this tool directly — the Voice Recorder captures only your microphone input. To record system audio (whatever is playing through your speakers — YouTube, Spotify, Zoom calls), use DocEasy's Screen Recorder tool instead. That tool uses the browser's getDisplayMedia API which can capture system audio along with screen video, and you can then extract just the audio using the Extract Audio tool. Alternatively, on Windows you can enable "Stereo Mix" in your sound settings and select it as the input device here — but this depends on your hardware drivers.

What happens if I close the tab while recording?

If you close the browser tab, refresh the page, or navigate away while recording, the recording is immediately cancelled and lost. There's no server-side recovery because everything happens in memory on your device. We strongly recommend clicking "Stop" and "Download" before closing the tab. For long recordings, set a reminder or keep the tab pinned so you don't accidentally close it. On mobile, avoid switching to other apps for long periods — some browsers may suspend background tabs, which could interrupt the recording.

Why is my recording file so large?

Voice recordings at 128 kbps are compact — a 10-minute recording is roughly 10 MB. If your file is much larger, it may be because your browser is saving at a higher bitrate (some save at 256 kbps or higher). To reduce file size, use DocEasy's Audio Converter to re-encode at 64 kbps — that's still perfectly clear for voice and halves the file size. For sharing on WhatsApp or email, aim for files under 25 MB. Music and singing benefit from 128 kbps or higher; spoken voice is fine at 64 kbps. Remember: halving the bitrate roughly halves the file size but also reduces quality.

Does the recorder work offline?

Yes — after you load this page once, the Voice Recorder continues working even without an internet connection. Since all audio processing happens locally in your browser, you can switch off Wi-Fi and still record. This is useful for flights, rural areas with poor connectivity, or privacy-conscious recording where you don't want any network activity. The only requirement is that the page itself has loaded once. If you want to record truly offline, open this page, wait for it to fully load, then turn off Wi-Fi. The recording will work normally, and downloading the file will still work since it's saved locally.

Why is my recording silent or barely audible?

If your recording plays back but is silent or very quiet, check these common causes. First, confirm the correct microphone is selected in your OS sound settings — if you have headphones plugged in with a mic, your laptop might be using that instead of the internal mic. Second, check your OS input volume slider — it should be at 70% or higher. Third, make sure no other app (Zoom, Teams, Discord, Skype) is holding the microphone exclusively — close them and refresh the page. Fourth, speak directly toward the mic, about 6–12 inches away. If the waveform barely moves during recording, the problem is at the input stage, not the recorder itself.

Can I convert the recording to MP3?

Yes — download your recording first (it'll be WebM, M4A, or OGG depending on your browser), then open DocEasy's Audio Converter tool and upload the file. Select MP3 as the output format and click Convert. In a few seconds you'll have an MP3 file that plays on literally any device, including old car stereos, MP3 players, and basic phones. The conversion happens locally in your browser using FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, so your file never leaves your device. This is useful when you need to upload the recording to a platform that only accepts MP3, or send it to someone with an old device.

Does the browser need to stay in focus while recording?

Not necessarily, but it's safer. On desktop Chrome and Edge, recording continues even if you switch to another tab or another application — the MediaRecorder keeps capturing in the background. On Firefox, recording generally continues too, but there have been reports of brief interruptions when the tab is heavily throttled. On mobile, background recording is less reliable — iOS Safari and Android Chrome may suspend background tabs after a few minutes to save battery. For critical recordings, keep this tab visible and your screen on. If you must switch away, do so briefly and check the waveform when you return.

Can I edit the recording after downloading?

Yes — after downloading, you can edit the recording with any audio editor. Free options include Audacity (Windows, Mac, Linux), Ocenaudio, or web-based tools. You can also use DocEasy's own tools for basic edits: use the Audio Splitter to cut a section, the Audio Speed Changer to adjust playback speed, or the Silence Remover to trim quiet gaps automatically. For more advanced editing like noise reduction, multi-track mixing, or effects, a desktop editor like Audacity is better. The recording file itself has no restrictions — you can open it, edit it, re-save it, or share it however you like.

Which browsers are supported?

DocEasy Voice Recorder works on all modern browsers: Google Chrome 90+, Microsoft Edge 90+, Mozilla Firefox 88+, Safari 14+, Brave, Opera, Vivaldi, and most Chromium-based browsers. Chrome and Edge offer the smoothest experience with WebM/Opus output and reliable pause/resume. Firefox works well too, though it saves as OGG. Safari on macOS and iOS supports the recorder but saves as M4A. If you're on an outdated browser (Internet Explorer, old Chrome below version 90), the "Start Recording" button may not work. The fix is simple — update your browser to the latest version, or switch to Chrome.
