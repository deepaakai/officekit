What is CGST, SGST, and IGST?

CGST (Central GST) and SGST (State GST) are charged when the seller and buyer are in the same state — each is typically half of the total GST rate. IGST (Integrated GST) is charged when the seller and buyer are in different states — it equals the full GST rate. Our tool automatically determines the correct tax type based on your state and the buyer's state.

2. What is GST and Why Does It Matter on Invoices?

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is India's unified indirect tax that replaced a patchwork of central and state taxes in 2017. Under GST, tax is charged based on where the seller and buyer are located:

  • Intra-state (same state): Tax is split into two halves — CGST (Central GST) and SGST (State GST). For example, an 18% GST becomes 9% CGST + 9% SGST.
  • Inter-state (different states): A single IGST (Integrated GST) is charged at the full rate — e.g., 18% IGST.
  • Union territories: Similar rules apply, with UTGST replacing SGST.

Getting the tax type right matters because your customer uses your invoice to claim Input Tax Credit (ITC). If you charge CGST+SGST when you should have charged IGST, the customer's ITC claim will be rejected, and you may face compliance issues.

The DocEasy generator determines the correct tax type automatically by comparing your company state with the buyer's state. If you want to override it — for example, for export invoices with zero tax — you can manually select the tax mode from the dropdown.

3. The Anatomy of a Professional GST Invoice

Every GST-compliant invoice must include the following fields. DocEasy's generator covers all of them:

  • Invoice number: A unique, sequential identifier (e.g., INV-2026-0001).
  • Invoice date: The date the invoice was issued.
  • Due date: The date payment is expected.
  • Seller details: Name, address, GSTIN, PAN, phone, email.
  • Buyer details: Name, address, GSTIN (for B2B transactions).
  • Place of supply: The state where the goods or services are delivered.
  • Item details: Description, HSN/SAC code, quantity, rate, discount, taxable value.
  • Tax breakdown: CGST, SGST, IGST amounts with rates.
  • Total: Grand total after tax and discount.
  • Amount in words: The invoice total written out in words.
  • Bank details: Account number, IFSC, branch for payment.
  • Terms & conditions: Payment terms, late fees, jurisdiction.
  • Authorized signatory: Signature and stamp.

5. How GST Calculation Works in This Tool

The calculation follows a straightforward, GST-compliant flow:

  1. Subtotal: Sum of (Quantity × Rate) for all line items.
  2. Discount: Applied to the subtotal. If percentage, the discount equals (Subtotal × Discount%). If fixed, it is subtracted directly.
  3. Taxable Amount: Subtotal − Discount.
  4. Tax: For each item, tax = (Item taxable value × GST rate). The total tax is then split into CGST+SGST (half each) or IGST (full) based on the tax mode.
  5. Shipping: Added after tax if enabled.
  6. Round Off: Optional rounding to the nearest rupee.
  7. Grand Total: Taxable Amount + Tax + Shipping + Round Off.

Every line item can have its own GST rate — for example, you could have one item at 5% GST, another at 12%, and another at 18%, and the tool will calculate each correctly and split them appropriately.

6. Adding Discounts and Extra Charges

Discounts are common in B2B invoicing, especially for early payment, bulk orders, or long-term clients. DocEasy supports two discount modes:

Shipping, delivery, and packaging charges are added after tax. Use the Shipping field to add these charges — useful for physical goods businesses.

12. Common Invoicing Mistakes to Avoid

  • Missing GSTIN: B2B invoices must include the buyer's GSTIN for them to claim ITC.
  • Wrong place of supply: Determines which state receives the tax revenue and affects ITC eligibility.
  • Incorrect HSN/SAC: Use the official code for your product or service.
  • Mismatched totals: Always verify subtotal, tax, and grand total add up correctly — DocEasy does this automatically.
  • Vague item descriptions: "Consulting services" is too vague — specify the scope.
  • No sequential numbering: Gaps and random numbers make audits painful.
