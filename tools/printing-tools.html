Everything about printing ID cards, photos, and documents at home — sizes, quality, and privacy.

What are DocEasy Printing Tools?

DocEasy's Printing Tools are a collection of free, browser-based utilities designed to prepare ID cards, Aadhaar copies, PAN cards, passport photos, signatures, and other documents for correct-size printing on A4 paper. Unlike a normal photo editor, these tools are purpose-built for the specific sizes required by government portals, banks, exam forms, and KYC verification. Each tool outputs print-ready images at 300 DPI — the standard resolution for professional printing. Nothing is uploaded to any server; all processing happens locally in your browser using HTML5 Canvas and JavaScript.

Is my Aadhaar or ID card data safe?

Completely safe. Every tool runs entirely client-side in your browser — nothing is ever uploaded to any server. You'll see zero outgoing requests carrying your data — because nothing is transmitted. This is a technical impossibility for us to see your files, not just a policy promise.

Why do I need 300 DPI for printing?

DPI (Dots Per Inch) determines the print quality of an image. Government portals, exam authorities, and banks usually specify 200–300 DPI for uploaded ID cards so that text, QR codes, and photos remain legible. At 72 DPI (typical screen resolution), text becomes blurry and QR codes stop scanning. At 300 DPI, every detail is razor-sharp even when printed at full size. DocEasy's printing tools automatically render your cards at 300 DPI by using the original PDF's vector data at up to 4× display resolution. If you upload a photo instead of a PDF, we upscale intelligently to preserve edges and reduce JPEG artifacts.

What is the correct Aadhaar card size for printing?

The standard Aadhaar card (like most Indian ID cards) follows the international CR80 standard — 8.5 × 5.5 cm (85.6 × 54 mm), the same size as a credit card. This is the size required by banks, SIM card vendors, and KYC verification portals. Our ID Card Dual-Side Cropper tool defaults to this size and outputs both front and back at exactly 8.5 × 5.5 cm on A4 paper. If a specific portal requires a different size (some ask for 8.6 × 5.4 cm for KYC), you can adjust the dimensions in the toolbar before printing.

How do I print a passport photo at home?

Use the Passport Photo Maker tool. It generates a 35 × 45 mm passport photo (standard Indian and most international sizes) at 300 DPI, arranged 8 times on a single A4 sheet. Print the sheet on glossy photo paper at 100% scale (never "fit to page"), then cut along the guides. You'll get 8 perfectly-sized passport photos for under ₹20 in printing costs — versus ₹100–200 at a studio. The tool also supports US (2×2 inch) and Schengen (35×45 mm) sizes, and you can adjust the number of copies per sheet.

What paper size is best for printing ID cards?

For ID cards (Aadhaar, PAN, etc.), regular A4 paper (210 × 297 mm) works perfectly for KYC submission. Print at 100% scale — never "fit to page" or "shrink to fit." For a more durable card, use 200 GSM matte cardstock or PVC sheets. For passport photos, use 180–250 GSM glossy photo paper. Printers with dye-sublimation or inkjet photo printers (Canon Pixma, HP Envy) produce the best photo quality. Laser printers work for ID documents but may have slightly less color accuracy for photos. Always verify your printer's scale setting is set to 100% before printing ID cards.

How do I remove the background from a signature?

Use the Signature BG Remover tool. Upload your scanned signature, adjust the threshold slider until the background is fully white and the signature lines are fully black, then download the cleaned PNG. What's the correct signature size for Indian exams?

Most Indian exam portals (SSC, UPSC, IBPS, RRB, NEET, JEE) require signatures at 140 × 60 pixels (4.5 × 2 cm) in JPG format, with file size between 10 KB and 50 KB. Some portals require slightly different sizes (some ask for 3.5 × 1.5 cm). Our Signature Resize tool lets you set exact pixel dimensions and target file size in KB — it automatically re-compresses the JPEG to hit your target. Just select "140 × 60 px" from the preset, set "Max 20 KB," and download. The output will be exactly what the portal expects.

Why is my printed Aadhaar card coming out too small or too big?

This is almost always a printer scaling issue. Most printers default to "Fit to Page" or "Shrink Oversized Pages," which automatically resizes your output to fit the margins — making a 8.5 × 5.5 cm ID card print at a different size. To fix: (1) In your browser's print dialog, click "More Settings"; (2) Set "Scale" to 100% (or "Actual Size"); (3) Uncheck "Fit to Printable Area"; (4) Make sure paper size is set to A4. After printing, measure the card with a ruler — if it's not exactly 8.5 × 5.5 cm, check the printer's own scaling settings (usually in printer properties). Some printers also have "Borderless" or "No Margins" modes that override default scaling.

How do I know my printed card will scan correctly?

Three things ensure scan-ability: (1) Print at 100% scale (never shrink); (2) Use at least 200 DPI output (our tools output 300 DPI by default); (3) Use good-quality paper — 100 GSM or higher. After printing, test with a smartphone camera or a QR code scanner: hold the printed card 6–8 inches from the lens and check if the QR code on Aadhaar or the barcode on PAN scans successfully. If the code doesn't scan, you may have printed too small — reprint at 100% scale. For Aadhaar's QR code, ensure the printed card is at least 8.5 cm wide for reliable scanning.

What if I don't have the original PDF, only a photo?

Photos work fine for most printing purposes. Take a well-lit, flat photo of your ID card (or use a scanner app), then upload it to the relevant tool. For best results: (1) Photograph on a flat matte surface (not glass or reflective); (2) Use natural daylight from a window rather than flash; (3) Hold the phone directly above the card (not at an angle); (4) Ensure the entire card is visible with a small margin.
