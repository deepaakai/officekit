How accurate is the Home Loan EMI Calculator?

The Home Loan EMI Calculator uses the standard compound interest formula prescribed by banks: EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1), where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate / 12 / 100), and n is the loan tenure in months. The calculation matches the output of major bank EMI calculators (HDFC, SBI, ICICI) to 2 decimal places. The tool also generates a detailed amortization schedule showing the split between principal and interest for each month — useful for understanding how much total interest you'll pay over the life of the loan. For loans with floating rates or prepayments, the calculation is an estimate — actual EMI may vary slightly based on the bank's day-count conventions.

What is the difference between old and new tax regimes?

India has two income tax regimes since April 2020. Old Regime: Higher tax rates but allows numerous deductions and exemptions — Section 80C (up to ₹1.5 lakh), 80D (health insurance), HRA exemption, home loan interest (Section 24), and standard deduction of ₹50,000. Best for people with significant investments and home loans. New Regime: Lower tax rates and wider slabs but no major deductions except standard deduction of ₹50,000 (₹75,000 from FY 2024-25). Best for people with fewer deductions or those who prefer simplicity. DocEasy's Income Tax Calculator compares both regimes side-by-side and tells you which one saves you more tax for your specific income. Choosing the right regime can save ₹10,000–50,000 per year.

How is in-hand salary calculated from CTC?

In-hand salary is your CTC minus all deductions. The main deductions are: (1) Employer PF contribution: 12% of basic salary (part of CTC but not paid to you directly). (2) Employee PF contribution: 12% of basic salary (deducted from your gross salary). (3) Professional Tax: ₹200 per month (varies by state). (4) Income Tax (TDS): As per your applicable tax slab, divided into 12 monthly deductions. (5) Gratuity: 4.81% of basic (part of CTC but paid only on exit). The rough formula: In-hand ≈ CTC × 0.75 to 0.85, depending on your tax bracket and structure. DocEasy's In-Hand Salary Calculator gives you an exact breakdown with all components itemized.

How does the HRA exemption work?

House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption under Section 10(13A) is the least of three values: (1) Actual HRA received from your employer; (2) Rent paid minus 10% of your basic salary; (3) 50% of basic salary (if living in metro cities — Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) or 40% of basic (non-metro). The exempted amount is subtracted from your taxable income, reducing your tax liability. For example, if your basic is ₹50,000/month, HRA is ₹20,000/month, and you pay ₹18,000/month rent in Delhi — the exemption is the minimum of ₹20,000, ₹18,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹13,000, and ₹25,000 → ₹13,000/month, which is ₹1,56,000/year tax-free. The HRA Exemption Calculator handles this automatically.

What is SIP and how do returns compound?

SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) is a method of investing a fixed amount in mutual funds at regular intervals — usually monthly. For example, investing ₹5,000 every month into an equity mutual fund. The power of SIP comes from rupee cost averaging and compounding. When markets are down, your fixed SIP buys more units; when markets are up, it buys fewer — averaging out your purchase cost over time. Compounding works by generating returns on your returns: a 12% annual return over 20 years on a ₹10,000 monthly SIP grows to about ₹99 lakh, of which ₹76 lakh is pure returns. The SIP Calculator projects this growth based on your monthly amount, expected return rate, and investment tenure. Returns are compounded monthly in the calculation.

How is gratuity calculated?

Gratuity is a lump-sum payment made by an employer to an employee who has completed at least 5 years of continuous service. The formula under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 is: Gratuity = (Last drawn salary × 15 × Years of service) / 26, where "last drawn salary" means basic + dearness allowance, and 26 represents the average number of working days in a month. For example, if your last drawn salary is ₹50,000 and you've worked 10 years: Gratuity = (50,000 × 15 × 10) / 26 = ₹2,88,461. Gratuity is tax-exempt up to ₹20 lakh (for non-government employees). The Gratuity Calculator handles the formula and shows your payable amount instantly, including the tax treatment.

What is EPF and how do I calculate my balance?

EPF (Employees' Provident Fund) is a retirement savings scheme where both you and your employer contribute 12% of your basic salary every month. Your contribution goes entirely to your EPF account, while your employer's contribution is split — 8.33% goes to EPS (pension) and 3.67% to EPF. The EPF balance grows with interest, currently around 8.25% per annum (revised annually by EPFO). For example, if your basic salary is ₹30,000/month and you work for 20 years at 8.25% interest, your EPF corpus could be roughly ₹45–55 lakh. The EPF Calculator projects this based on your current salary, expected salary growth, and interest rate. Your actual EPF balance is available on the EPFO member portal (unifiedportal-mem.epfindia.gov.in).

How do I calculate the best loan tenure?

Shorter tenure means higher EMI but lower total interest — longer tenure means lower EMI but higher total interest. For example, on a ₹50 lakh home loan at 8.5% interest: 15-year tenure → EMI ₹49,233, total interest ₹38.6 lakh. 20-year tenure → EMI ₹43,391, total interest ₹54.1 lakh. 25-year tenure → EMI ₹40,247, total interest ₹70.7 lakh. The "best" tenure depends on your cash flow — choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford. A common rule of thumb: EMI should not exceed 40% of your monthly take-home salary. Our Home Loan EMI Calculator lets you compare tenures side-by-side to find your sweet spot.

Can I plan prepayments to save interest?

Yes — our loan calculators include an optional extra monthly payment field. When you make a prepayment, you have two choices: reduce tenure (keep EMI same) or reduce EMI (keep tenure same). Reducing tenure saves more interest — this is almost always the better financial choice. For example, on a ₹50 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 20 years, prepaying ₹5 lakh in year 5 and choosing tenure reduction saves about ₹15 lakh in interest over the loan's life. If you choose EMI reduction instead, you save about ₹6 lakh. The Home Loan EMI Calculator lets you model prepayments and see the savings in real-time.
