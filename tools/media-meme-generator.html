Why the Classic Impact Font?

The classic meme format uses Impact font with ALL CAPS text, white fill, and black stroke, positioned at the top and bottom of the image. This style originated on 4chan's /b/ board in the mid-2000s with "Advice Animals" and spread to Reddit, Imgur, and every social platform. Impact is important because it's bold, condensed, and highly readable at small sizes — even on phone screens. The white-on-black stroke ensures visibility against any background — light, dark, or busy. DocEasy's Meme Generator defaults to this style, so you can literally just type and download.

Customization Options

  • Font family: Impact (classic), Arial (clean), Comic Sans (playful), Georgia (serif), Courier New (mono).
  • Font size: 20–120 pixels. For a 1200×800 image, 60–80 px works well. For a 1920×1080 screenshot, use 80–100 px.
  • Text color: White is standard, but any color works — try yellow for emphasis, red for warnings.
  • Stroke color: Black is standard. Use a contrasting color for creative effects.
  • Stroke width: 0–10 px. Thicker strokes work better on busy backgrounds; thinner strokes on clean backgrounds.
  • Uppercase: Toggle ON for classic style, OFF for a modern look with proper capitalization.

Format and Quality

The output is always a PNG file at your image's original resolution. PNG is lossless, supports millions of colors, and works everywhere. If you upload a 4K image, your meme is 4K. If you upload a 500×500 image, that's what you get. We recommend 800 pixels wide minimum for crisp results on social media. For Instagram square posts, use 1080×1080. For Twitter cards, 1600×900.

Privacy and Security

Your images are never recorded, saved, or transmitted. The Meme Generator uses the browser's HTML5 Canvas API to render the meme entirely on your device. When you drop an image, it's read into memory using FileReader. When you click Download, the canvas is exported to PNG using canvas.toBlob(). At no point does any data travel to DocEasy's servers. You can verify this yourself by opening Developer Tools (F12), going to the Network tab, and watching — you'll see zero outgoing requests carrying your image.
