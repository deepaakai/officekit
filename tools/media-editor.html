Universal Media Editor — 12 Tools in One Page (2026)

DocEasy's Universal Media Editor packs 12 professional video and audio tools into a single browser page — no install, no signup, no watermark, and complete privacy. Whether you need to trim a clip for social media, compress a video for WhatsApp, extract audio for a podcast, or convert formats for compatibility, this tool covers it all with a single upload.

The Universal Media Editor shows all 12 tools as tabs — pick one, drop your file, and process locally in your browser.

What Are the 12 Tools?

Instead of navigating to 12 different pages, the Universal Media Editor consolidates everything into a single tabbed interface:

  • 1. Trim — Cut video or audio to exact start and end times. Lossless for MP4/H.264.
  • 2. Mute — Remove the audio track completely while keeping video untouched.
  • 3. Compress — Reduce file size with three CRF presets (Light, Balanced, Strong).
  • 4. Resize — Change resolution to any width × height (e.g., 4K → 720p).
  • 5. Rotate — Turn video 90°, 180°, or 270° to fix phone orientation.
  • 6. Flip — Mirror horizontally or vertically for creative or corrective effects.
  • 7. Speed — Speed up or slow down video from 0.25× to 4×. Audio pitch preserved.
  • 8. Loop — Repeat the video 2–10 times into a single continuous file.
  • 9. Reverse — Play video and audio backwards (great for short clips).
  • 10. Extract Audio — Pull the audio track as MP3, WAV, OGG, or M4A.
  • 11. Convert — Change format to MP4, WebM, animated GIF, or MP3.
  • 12. Extract Frames — Export JPG stills at custom frame rates.

How to Use — Step by Step

  1. Step 1 — Pick a tool: Click any tab at the top (Trim, Mute, Compress, etc.).
  2. Step 2 — Drop your file: Drag-and-drop video or audio into the drop zone, or click to browse.
  3. Step 3 — Configure options: Each tool shows its own options below the drop zone (e.g., timestamps for Trim, CRF preset for Compress).
  4. Step 4 — Click "Process File": The FFmpeg WebAssembly engine loads once (~10 seconds) then processes locally.
  5. Step 5 — Preview and download: Watch the result and click "Download Result" to save it.

That's it — no signup, no email, no watermark. The entire workflow runs in your browser tab.

Why Use a Browser-Based Editor?

Traditional video editing requires installing heavy desktop software (Adobe Premiere, DaVinci Resolve) or uploading files to cloud services (Kapwing, CloudConvert, Loom). Both have drawbacks:

  • Desktop software: Takes minutes to install, requires admin rights, consumes gigabytes of disk space, and often has a steep learning curve.
  • Cloud services: Requires uploading potentially sensitive files to a third-party server, often with file-size limits, watermarks, or paywalls.

DocEasy's Universal Media Editor takes a different approach: it uses FFmpeg compiled to WebAssembly, which runs the same professional-grade video engine directly inside your browser. Your files never leave your device. And because WebAssembly runs at near-native speed, processing is fast — often within 1–3 seconds for simple operations.

Trim: Cut Any Video to Exact Time

The Trim tool is the fastest way to cut a video clip. Enter your start time (00:00:00 or seconds) and end time (00:02:45), click Process, and download. For MP4/H.264 files, the trim is lossless — we cut at keyframe boundaries without re-encoding, which means no quality loss and instant processing (typically 1–3 seconds).

Mute: Remove Audio Without Re-Encoding

Mute strips the audio track completely while leaving the video codec untouched. This is useful when you want to reuse a video clip with new music, or when you want a silent version for a kiosk or background video. Because we use stream copy (-c:v copy), the operation is virtually instant.

Compress: Reduce File Size for Sharing

Compression uses H.264 with adjustable CRF (Constant Rate Factor):

  • Light (CRF 28): Best quality, ~30–50% size reduction.
  • Balanced (CRF 32): Recommended for social media — 50–70% size reduction, minimal visible loss.
  • Strong (CRF 36): Maximum compression — 70–85% reduction, visible artifacts.

Combine with Resize (e.g., 4K → 1080p) for dramatic size savings — a 500 MB video often shrinks to under 50 MB.

Resize, Rotate, Flip — Fix Orientation Issues

Videos filmed on phones often end up sideways or upside-down. The Rotate tool fixes orientation in one click (90°, 180°, 270°). The Flip tool mirrors horizontally (useful for un-mirroring selfie videos) or vertically. The Resize tool changes resolution to any exact dimensions — great for uploading to platforms with specific requirements.

Speed, Loop, Reverse — Creative Effects

Speed lets you create timelapses (0.25× fast-forward) or slow-motion (up to 4× slower) with audio pitch preserved. Loop repeats a video 2–10 times into a single file — perfect for animated backgrounds or repetitive content. Reverse plays everything backwards — great for short clips, humorous effects, or reversing a specific action for dramatic effect.

Extract Audio — Video to MP3

Extract Audio pulls the sound from any video file and saves it as MP3, WAV, OGG, or M4A. Choose your bitrate (64 kbps for voice, 192–320 kbps for music), click Process, and download. This is the fastest way to convert a lecture or interview video into a portable audio file.

Convert — Format Conversion

The Convert tool handles the most common format conversions:

  • MP4: Universal compatibility (H.264 + AAC).
  • WebM: Modern, efficient, royalty-free (VP9 + Opus).
  • GIF: Animated image (limited to 256 colors, no audio).
  • MP3: Audio-only extraction.

Extract Frames — Video to JPG Stills

The Frames tool exports JPG images from any video at a chosen frame rate (1–10 fps) with a maximum frame count. Use it for thumbnails, storyboards, preview images, or extracting a specific moment from a video.

Privacy and Security

Unlike cloud-based editors that require uploading your files to a third-party server, DocEasy's Universal Media Editor processes everything locally:

  • No uploads: Files stay in your browser's memory.
  • No server storage: Nothing is saved anywhere except your device.
  • No tracking: We don't log which files you process or when.
  • No account: Use it completely anonymously.
  • Offline capable: After the FFmpeg engine loads once, the tool works offline.

You can verify the privacy claim yourself: open Developer Tools (F12) → Network tab, drop a file, and click Process. You'll see the FFmpeg engine download (first time only) but zero requests carrying your file data — because it never travels over the network.

Best Practices

  • Close other tabs before processing large files (above 200 MB) to free RAM.
  • Use Chrome or Edge for the best WebAssembly performance.
  • Trim first, then compress — smaller files process faster.
  • Use MP4 for universal compatibility, WebM for modern browsers.
  • Choose 128 kbps for voice, 192–320 kbps for music extraction.
  • Test with a short clip before processing a full-length video.

Input: MP4, WebM, MOV, MKV, AVI, FLV, TS, M4V, 3GP (video); MP3, WAV, OGG, OPUS, M4A, AAC, FLAC, WMA (audio). Output: MP4, WebM, GIF, MP3, WAV, OGG, M4A, JPG (frames).

How do I trim a video to a specific time?

Click the Trim tab, enter start and end times in HH:MM:SS format (or plain seconds), and click Process. For MP4/H.264, the trim is lossless — no quality loss.

Does compressing a video reduce quality?

Depends on the preset. "Light" (CRF 28) keeps most quality, "Balanced" (CRF 32) is best for social media, and "Strong" (CRF 36) reduces size dramatically with visible artifacts.

How do I extract audio as MP3?

Click the Audio tab, select MP3 and 192 kbps (music) or 128 kbps (voice), drop your video, click Process. You'll get an MP3 file containing only the audio track.

Can I convert video to GIF?

Yes — click Convert, choose "Animated GIF," and click Process. GIFs are limited to 256 colors and don't support audio. Best for short clips under 10 seconds.

Simple operations (trim, mute, rotate, flip) take 1–3 seconds. Compress, resize, and convert take 10–60 seconds for a 100 MB file. The first operation also includes a one-time ~10-second FFmpeg engine load.
